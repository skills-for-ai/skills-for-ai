# Mumps Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Mumps is a contagious viral infection best known for causing painful
swelling of the salivary glands, particularly the parotid glands near
the jaw. It's preventable with vaccination (MMR).

## Common symptoms

Swelling and tenderness of the salivary glands (giving a characteristic
"chipmunk cheeks" appearance), fever, headache, muscle aches, fatigue,
and pain when chewing or swallowing.

## General self-care measures

- Rest and stay well hydrated.
- Use pain/fever-reducing medication as appropriate for age and health
  status.
- Apply warm or cold compresses to swollen glands for comfort.
- Eat soft foods that don't require much chewing, and avoid acidic
  foods/drinks that can increase salivary gland discomfort.
- Isolate from others while contagious to prevent spread, particularly
  from unvaccinated individuals.

## When to seek medical attention

- Suspected mumps, for diagnosis and to discuss isolation precautions.
- Severe headache, stiff neck, or confusion (can indicate a rare but
  serious complication affecting the brain or its lining) — see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Testicular pain and swelling in a male past puberty (a recognized
  mumps complication needing prompt evaluation).
- Abdominal pain (can indicate pancreatic involvement).

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming mumps is no longer a concern due to vaccination programs**
  — outbreaks still occur, including in vaccinated populations in some
  circumstances; symptoms consistent with mumps still warrant
  evaluation.
- **Continuing normal social contact while contagious** — mumps spreads
  via respiratory droplets and saliva; isolation during the contagious
  period matters for protecting others.
- **Overlooking complications beyond the salivary glands** — mumps can
  occasionally affect other organs (testes, ovaries, pancreas, brain
  lining); new symptoms beyond the classic swelling deserve attention.

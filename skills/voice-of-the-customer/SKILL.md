# Voice of the Customer

Voice of the Customer (VoC) is the process of capturing customer
feedback, opinions, preferences, and needs regarding a product or
service — a way to understand customers well enough to make informed
decisions about how to actually meet their needs, rather than guessing.

## Capture channels

Surveys, focus groups, customer support interactions, social media, and
other feedback mechanisms — different channels surface different kinds
of insight: support interactions reveal acute pain points in real usage;
surveys can quantify sentiment across a broad population; social media
surfaces unprompted, unfiltered reaction. Relying on only one channel
means missing whatever kind of insight the others would have caught.

## Benefits

- **Improved customer satisfaction** — understanding actual wants/needs
  lets an organization make the right improvements, not just any
  improvement.
- **Increased customer loyalty** — customers who see their feedback
  listened to and acted on build stronger relationships with the
  organization, improving retention.
- **Enhanced product development** — feedback-driven development is more
  likely to produce something that actually succeeds in the market than
  development driven purely by internal assumption.
- **Better decision-making** — a clear picture of what customers actually
  want informs where to invest resources and how to prioritize, rather
  than prioritizing by internal opinion or loudest voice in the room.

## Where VoC feeds into other frameworks

VoC data is a primary, grounding input for [[product-management]]'s
discovery process, for choosing a [[north-star]] metric that
reflects genuine customer value rather than a vanity number, and for
[[user-centred-design]]'s research methods more broadly — VoC and
UCD overlap heavily; VoC leans toward capturing feedback about an
existing or proposed offering specifically, while UCD's research methods
span the whole design process including problems not yet framed as a
product at all.

## Common pitfalls

- **Collecting feedback through only one channel** — see above; each
  channel has real, different blind spots.
- **Capturing feedback but never acting on it** — undermines the whole
  point, and specifically damages the "customers see they're heard"
  loyalty benefit if feedback is solicited and then visibly ignored.
- **Treating the loudest or most frequent feedback as most representative**
  — vocal customers (especially in support channels or social media) can
  skew perception away from the quieter majority's actual experience;
  triangulate across channels rather than over-weighting the loudest one.
- **Confusing VoC with market research generally** — VoC specifically
  centers actual customers' direct feedback about real experience, not
  broader market/competitor analysis (see [[pestle-analysis]],
  [[swot-analysis]] for that different, complementary discipline).

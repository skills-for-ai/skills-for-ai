# Medical Language Speaking Assessment for English

Clinical English-language speaking assessment for internationally
educated healthcare professionals, modelled on the Occupational English
Test (OET) Medicine speaking sub-test, using role-played patient
scenarios and criterion-based scoring on linguistic and clinical
communication indicators.

## Scoring

**Instrument:** OET Speaking Sub-test (Medicine profession). Range
0–500 raw, graded:

- **A (450–500)** — high-level professional proficiency (pass for UK
  healthcare registration).
- **B (350–440)** — good professional proficiency (pass).
- **C+ / C (200–340)** — functional proficiency; further practice
  required for many regulators.
- **D / E (0–190)** — below functional threshold.

## What it covers

Candidate details; a role-play patient interview; a role-play clinical
explanation; linguistic criteria rating; and clinical communication
indicators with an overall grade.

## Common pitfalls

- **Grading purely on grammatical accuracy** — the OET-style rubric
  explicitly weighs clinical communication indicators (empathy,
  clarity of explanation, appropriate structure) alongside linguistic
  criteria; strong grammar with poor clinical communication style
  doesn't earn a top grade.
- **Applying pass thresholds inconsistently across regulators** — which
  grade counts as a "pass" can differ by the specific regulatory body a
  candidate is registering with; verify the applicable threshold rather
  than assuming Grade B is universally sufficient.
- **Confusing this with the Welsh-language assessment** — see
  [[medical-language-speaking-assessment-for-cymraeg]]; the two use
  different frameworks and scoring ranges and aren't directly
  comparable.

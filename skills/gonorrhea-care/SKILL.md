# Gonorrhea Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Gonorrhea is a common bacterial sexually transmitted infection. It's
treatable with antibiotics, but untreated infection can lead to
serious complications, including infertility.

## Common symptoms

Painful urination, unusual discharge, and (in some people) pelvic or
testicular pain. Many people, especially women, have no noticeable
symptoms at all, which is part of why it can go undiagnosed and spread.

## General self-care measures

- Complete the full prescribed course of antibiotics exactly as
  directed, even if symptoms improve quickly.
- Avoid sexual contact until treatment is complete and confirmed
  effective, to avoid re-infection or spreading it further.
- Notify sexual partners so they can be tested and treated —
  many health services offer confidential partner-notification
  support.
- Get tested for other sexually transmitted infections, since
  co-infection is common.

## When to seek medical attention

- Any symptoms suggestive of an STI, or after a potential exposure —
  testing doesn't require waiting for symptoms.
- Pelvic pain, fever, or symptoms suggesting the infection has spread
  (can indicate pelvic inflammatory disease or another complication).
- Symptoms persisting after completing treatment.

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming no symptoms means no infection** — gonorrhea is often
  asymptomatic, particularly in women; regular testing based on risk,
  not symptoms alone, is the more reliable approach.
- **Stopping antibiotics early once symptoms improve** — incomplete
  treatment risks treatment failure and contributes to antibiotic
  resistance.
- **Not notifying partners** — untreated partners can re-infect the
  person after treatment and continue spreading the infection.

# Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)

Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) is a type of software system that
lets organizations manage their business processes and operations in an
integrated, centralized manner — a comprehensive suite of tools
automating and streamlining operations across functions like finance,
accounting, human resources, supply chain management, and customer
relationship management.

## How ERP systems are structured

Typically a database, a set of integrated applications built on top of
it, and a user interface for accessing and interacting with the data and
applications — designed to provide real-time information, automate
workflows, and surface insights supporting better-informed decisions.

## Key benefit: breaking down functional silos

One of ERP's central advantages is enabling cross-functional
collaboration by giving all business units and departments access to the
same underlying data on the same platform, rather than each department
running its own disconnected system. This also tends to improve
efficiency, reduce costs, and improve customer satisfaction through more
timely and accurate information.

## Deployment and customization

ERP systems can be customized to an organization's specific needs and
deployed on-premise or in the cloud. Implementation is typically a
complex, time-consuming undertaking requiring careful planning, testing,
and training — but once in place, a well-implemented ERP system can
meaningfully improve overall business performance.

## Common pitfalls

- **Underestimating implementation complexity** — ERP rollouts are
  substantial change-management undertakings, not just a software
  install; see [[enterprise-change-management]] for the structured
  approach a rollout of this scale typically needs.
- **Over-customizing the system** — extensive customization away from an
  ERP vendor's standard configuration can make future upgrades far more
  difficult and expensive.
- **Insufficient training** — an ERP system that centralizes data across
  departments only delivers its benefit if the people in those
  departments actually know how to use it correctly.
- **Treating ERP selection as purely a technical decision** — the choice
  of ERP system has deep implications for business processes across the
  whole organization; it warrants the same kind of structured,
  stakeholder-inclusive evaluation as other major enterprise
  investments.

# Malnutrition Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Malnutrition means a deficiency, excess, or imbalance in a person's
nutrient intake, most commonly discussed in the context of
under-nutrition — not getting enough calories, protein, or specific
nutrients.

## Common symptoms

Unintentional weight loss, fatigue, muscle weakness, slow wound
healing, frequent infections, and — in more advanced cases — noticeable
loss of muscle and fat.

## General self-care measures

- Eat regular, nutritionally balanced meals; a dietitian can help
  design an appropriate plan for a specific situation.
- Address underlying causes where identifiable (swallowing difficulty,
  digestive conditions, appetite loss from illness or medication).
- Use nutritional supplements only as recommended by a healthcare
  professional, since inappropriate supplementation (especially
  reintroducing food too quickly after severe malnutrition) can be
  harmful.
- Monitor weight and nutritional status over time, especially for
  those at higher risk (older adults, people with chronic illness).

## When to seek medical attention

- Unintentional weight loss, especially if significant or unexplained.
- Signs of malnutrition in someone with a chronic illness, difficulty
  eating, or reduced appetite over time.
- Concerns about a child not gaining weight or growing as expected.
- Severe malnutrition — refeeding needs careful medical supervision
  due to the risk of refeeding syndrome.

## Common pitfalls

- **Reintroducing food or nutrition too quickly after a period of
  severe malnutrition** — this risks refeeding syndrome, a potentially
  serious complication; nutritional support after significant
  malnutrition needs professional guidance on pacing.
- **Assuming malnutrition only affects visibly underweight people** —
  it can occur in people of any body size, including those who appear
  overweight, if their diet lacks essential nutrients.
- **Treating unintentional weight loss as automatically positive** —
  unlike planned weight loss, unintentional weight loss (particularly in
  older adults or those with chronic illness) is often a warning sign
  worth investigating.

# Dehydration Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Dehydration occurs when the body loses more fluid than it takes in,
impairing normal function. It can range from mild to
life-threatening, and some groups (infants, older adults, people who
are ill) are at higher risk.

## Common symptoms

Thirst, dry mouth, dark yellow urine, fatigue, dizziness, headache, and
— in more severe dehydration — confusion, rapid heartbeat, sunken eyes,
and reduced urination.

## General self-care measures

- Drink water or an oral rehydration solution regularly, especially
  during illness (vomiting, diarrhoea, fever), heat exposure, or
  intense physical activity.
- Increase fluid intake proactively during hot weather or illness
  rather than waiting until thirsty.
- Monitor urine colour as a simple hydration indicator (pale yellow is
  generally a good sign; dark yellow suggests more fluid is needed).
- Be especially attentive with infants, young children, and older
  adults, who dehydrate more easily and show symptoms differently.

## When to seek medical attention

- Signs of severe dehydration: confusion, very reduced or no urination,
  rapid heartbeat, sunken eyes, or lethargy — seek urgent care; see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Inability to keep fluids down due to vomiting.
- Dehydration in an infant, young child, or older adult, who can
  deteriorate faster than a healthy adult.
- Dehydration alongside diarrhoea/vomiting lasting more than a day or
  two, especially with fever.

## Common pitfalls

- **Waiting until feeling thirsty to drink** — thirst is a lagging
  indicator; by the time it's noticeable, some dehydration has often
  already occurred.
- **Treating severe dehydration with plain water alone** — significant
  fluid loss (from vomiting, diarrhoea, heavy sweating) also loses
  electrolytes; an oral rehydration solution replaces both.
- **Underestimating dehydration risk in infants and older adults** —
  both groups dehydrate faster and show different warning signs than a
  healthy adult; err toward earlier medical evaluation.

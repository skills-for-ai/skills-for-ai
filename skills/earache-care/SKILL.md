# Earache Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Earache (ear pain) is commonly caused by ear infections (especially in
children), fluid buildup, earwax blockage, or referred pain from
nearby structures (teeth, jaw, throat).

## Common symptoms

Ear pain (dull, sharp, or throbbing), a feeling of fullness or pressure
in the ear, reduced hearing, and — with infection — fever or fluid
discharge.

## General self-care measures

- Use over-the-counter pain relief as appropriate for the person's age
  and health status.
- Apply a warm compress to the affected ear for comfort.
- Avoid inserting anything into the ear canal (including cotton
  swabs), which can worsen irritation or push earwax further in.
- Keep the ear dry, especially if there's any discharge or a known
  perforation risk.

## When to seek medical attention

- Severe pain, high fever, or symptoms lasting more than a couple of
  days.
- Discharge (especially blood or pus) from the ear.
- Hearing loss or dizziness alongside ear pain.
- Recurrent ear infections, especially in children.
- Swelling or redness behind the ear, or a stiff neck with earache
  (can indicate a more serious complication) — see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]].

## Common pitfalls

- **Inserting cotton swabs or other objects into the ear canal** to
  relieve discomfort — this commonly worsens the problem by pushing
  wax deeper or damaging the ear canal or eardrum.
- **Assuming all earache is infection requiring antibiotics** — many
  cases (viral, wax-related, or referred pain) don't need antibiotics;
  a professional evaluation determines the actual cause.
- **Ignoring recurrent ear infections in children** — frequent
  infections can affect hearing and development and warrant specific
  evaluation, not repeated one-off treatment.

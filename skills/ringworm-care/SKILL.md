# Ringworm Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Ringworm is a common fungal skin infection (not caused by a worm,
despite the name), causing a characteristic ring-shaped rash. It's
contagious and can spread through direct contact or shared items.

## Common symptoms

A red, itchy, ring- or circle-shaped rash with a raised, scaly border
and often clearer skin in the center, which can appear on the body,
scalp, feet (athlete's foot), or groin (jock itch).

## General self-care measures

- Use an antifungal cream, powder, or shampoo as directed, continuing
  for the full recommended duration even after the rash appears to
  clear.
- Keep the affected area clean and dry — fungus thrives in warm, moist
  environments.
- Avoid sharing towels, clothing, or grooming items while infected.
- Wash bedding and clothing that's been in contact with the affected
  area.

## When to seek medical attention

- A rash that doesn't improve with over-the-counter antifungal
  treatment after a couple of weeks.
- Ringworm on the scalp, which often needs oral (not just topical)
  antifungal treatment.
- Widespread or recurrent ringworm, or ringworm in someone with a
  weakened immune system.
- Signs of secondary bacterial infection (increasing redness, warmth,
  pus).

## Common pitfalls

- **Stopping treatment as soon as the rash looks better** — fungal
  infections often need treatment continued for longer than symptoms
  visibly persist, or they can recur.
- **Treating scalp ringworm with topical cream alone** — scalp
  infections typically need oral antifungal medication, since topical
  treatment doesn't penetrate hair follicles effectively.
- **Continuing to share towels or clothing during treatment** — this
  risks spreading the infection to others or reinfecting the same
  person.

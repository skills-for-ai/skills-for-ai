# Tennis Elbow Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis) is a condition causing pain on the
outer part of the elbow, caused by overuse or strain of the tendons
that attach forearm muscles to the elbow — despite the name, it commonly
affects people who don't play tennis.

## Common symptoms

Pain or burning on the outer elbow, sometimes radiating into the
forearm, weakened grip strength, and pain worsened by gripping,
twisting, or lifting with the wrist extended.

## General self-care measures

- Rest the affected arm from the aggravating activity, at least
  temporarily.
- Apply ice for pain and inflammation, especially after activity.
- Use a forearm strap/brace if recommended, to reduce strain on the
  tendon during activity.
- Do targeted stretching and strengthening exercises, ideally guided by
  a physiotherapist, once acute pain has settled.
- Modify technique or ergonomics in the activity that caused it
  (sports technique, workstation setup, repetitive tasks).

## When to seek medical attention

- Pain persisting despite rest and self-care over several weeks.
- Significant weakness or an inability to grip normally.
- Symptoms interfering with work or daily activities.

## Common pitfalls

- **Continuing the aggravating activity without modification** — this
  is one of the most common reasons tennis elbow becomes chronic rather
  than resolving in a reasonable timeframe.
- **Resting completely without any structured rehabilitation** — while
  initial rest matters, appropriate strengthening exercises (introduced
  at the right time) are generally part of effective recovery, not
  purely avoidance.
- **Assuming it will resolve immediately once the aggravating activity
  stops** — tennis elbow often takes weeks to months to fully resolve,
  even with appropriate management.

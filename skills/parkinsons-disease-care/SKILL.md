# Parkinson's Disease Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Parkinson's disease is a progressive neurological condition affecting
movement, caused by the loss of dopamine-producing brain cells.

## Common symptoms

Tremor (often starting in one hand), slowness of movement (bradykinesia),
muscle stiffness, balance and coordination problems, and — for some —
non-movement symptoms such as sleep disturbance, mood changes, and
changes in sense of smell.

## General self-care measures

- Take medication on a consistent schedule — timing matters
  significantly for symptom control in Parkinson's.
- Stay physically active; specific exercise types (balance, strength,
  flexibility) are commonly recommended by care teams.
- Address swallowing or speech changes early with the appropriate
  specialist support.
- Adapt the home environment for fall prevention (see
  [[fall-risk-assessment]]).

## When to seek medical attention

- A sudden worsening of symptoms or new symptoms.
- Signs of medication complications (unusual movements, hallucinations,
  significant mood changes).
- A fall, especially with injury.
- See [[urgent-emergency-care]] for any sudden severe symptom.

## Common pitfalls

- **Taking medication inconsistently or late** — Parkinson's medication
  timing directly affects symptom control; even short delays can matter.
- **Underestimating fall risk** — balance problems increase fall risk
  significantly; proactive prevention matters more than reacting after
  a fall.
- **Overlooking non-movement symptoms** — mood, sleep, and cognitive
  changes are a real part of Parkinson's and deserve attention alongside
  movement symptoms.

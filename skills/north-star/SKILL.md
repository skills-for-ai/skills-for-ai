# North Star

A "North Star" is a singular, overarching goal or objective that guides a
company's decision-making and strategy — the guiding principle that keeps
an organization focused on what matters most and drives it toward its
long-term vision.

## Why it matters in agile/lean methodologies

Common in agile and lean startup practice as a tool for staying focused
under uncertainty, avoiding distraction, and making effective decisions
without needing to re-derive priorities from scratch each time. A clear
North Star lets an organization align effort, sustain motivation, and
measure progress toward its ultimate goal without every decision needing
a full strategic debate.

## The North Star metric

For many companies, the North Star is expressed as a specific, trackable
metric (or small set of metrics) the company optimizes — customer
satisfaction, revenue growth, market share, or something more specific to
the business model. The defining example: Airbnb's North Star metric is
"nights booked" — a single number that captures whether the platform is
actually succeeding at connecting travelers with accommodation, letting
the company make data-driven decisions and stay focused on its mission
without needing a broader, harder-to-track proxy for success.

## Choosing a good North Star metric

A strong North Star metric should be tied directly to the company's
mission and vision (see [[vision-mission-values]]) and should
represent genuine customer/business value, not just an easily-measured
proxy — a metric optimized in isolation from real value delivered risks
becoming a vanity number (see [[key-performance-indicators]]'s
identical warning about vanity metrics, and
[[objectives-and-key-results]]'s "as measured by" discipline for
the same underlying reason).

## Relationship to other strategic frameworks

- **[[big-hairy-audacious-goal]]** — a BHAG is a longer-horizon
  (10–30 year), more aspirational statement of ultimate purpose; a North
  Star is typically a nearer-term, directly trackable metric that
  operationalizes progress toward that larger purpose.
- **[[objectives-and-key-results]]** — OKRs are often set to move
  the North Star metric specifically, giving quarterly/short-term
  execution a clear line back to the single overarching goal.
- **[[strategic-balanced-scorecard]]** — a North Star can anchor the
  destination statement a balanced scorecard builds its four perspectives
  around.

## Common pitfalls

- **Picking a metric that's easy to move but doesn't reflect real value**
  — the same vanity-metric trap [[key-performance-indicators]]
  warns against generally, applied at the highest strategic level where
  it does the most damage if wrong.
- **Multiple competing "North Stars"** — the whole point is a *single*
  overarching focus; several simultaneous North Star metrics reintroduce
  the prioritization ambiguity the concept exists to resolve.
- **Setting a North Star and never revisiting it** as the business model
  evolves — a metric that made sense at an earlier stage (e.g. signups)
  can stop reflecting real value as the company matures (e.g. toward
  retention or revenue).

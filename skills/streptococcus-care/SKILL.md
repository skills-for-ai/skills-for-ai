# Streptococcus Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Streptococcus bacteria cause a range of infections, most commonly
"strep throat" (streptococcal pharyngitis), but also skin infections
and, rarely, more serious invasive infections.

## Common symptoms

Sudden sore throat, pain swallowing, fever, red and swollen tonsils
(sometimes with white patches), swollen neck glands, and headache —
notably, strep throat typically does *not* cause a cough, which helps
distinguish it from a viral sore throat.

## General self-care measures

- Complete the full prescribed course of antibiotics exactly as
  directed, even after symptoms improve.
- Rest and stay hydrated; warm liquids and salt-water gargling can ease
  throat discomfort.
- Use pain/fever-reducing medication as appropriate.
- Replace your toothbrush after starting antibiotics to avoid
  reinfection.
- Stay home until at least 24 hours after starting antibiotics, since
  strep is contagious before treatment takes effect.

## When to seek medical attention

- Sore throat with fever and no cough, especially with white patches on
  the tonsils — get tested, since strep throat needs antibiotic
  treatment (unlike most sore throats).
- Difficulty breathing or swallowing, drooling, or a muffled voice —
  see [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Skin infection with spreading redness, warmth, or fever.
- Symptoms not improving within a couple of days of starting
  antibiotics.

## Common pitfalls

- **Stopping antibiotics early once feeling better** — untreated or
  incompletely treated strep can, rarely, lead to serious complications
  (including rheumatic fever); completing the full course matters.
- **Assuming a sore throat with cough is strep** — the presence of a
  cough makes a viral cause more likely; testing (not guessing) is the
  reliable way to distinguish strep from viral sore throat.
- **Not replacing a toothbrush after starting treatment** — a
  toothbrush used while infected can harbor bacteria and contribute to
  reinfection.

# Panic Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

A panic attack is a sudden episode of intense fear with strong physical
symptoms, often peaking within minutes. Recurrent, unexpected panic
attacks with ongoing worry about future attacks may indicate panic
disorder.

## Common symptoms

Rapid heartbeat, chest pain or tightness, shortness of breath,
trembling, sweating, dizziness, a feeling of choking or losing control,
and a sense of impending doom — often intensely frightening despite not
being physically dangerous in itself.

## General self-care measures

- Practice slow, controlled breathing during an attack — many people
  find focusing on a slow exhale particularly helpful.
- Ground yourself using sensory techniques (naming things you can see,
  hear, touch) to interrupt escalating fear.
- Remind yourself that a panic attack, while frightening, typically
  peaks and subsides within minutes and is not itself dangerous.
- Work with a therapist on longer-term strategies if attacks recur,
  since avoidance of triggering situations often worsens panic disorder
  over time.

## When to seek medical attention

- A first panic attack, especially since chest pain and other physical
  symptoms can overlap with a genuine medical emergency and should be
  evaluated, particularly the first time.
- Recurrent panic attacks affecting daily life or causing avoidance
  behaviour.
- Physical symptoms that don't fit a typical panic attack pattern —
  when uncertain, seek urgent evaluation; see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]].

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming chest pain during a first episode is definitely a panic
  attack** — cardiac and panic symptoms can overlap significantly;
  a first episode especially warrants medical evaluation to rule out
  a physical cause.
- **Avoiding places or situations associated with a past panic attack**
  — avoidance tends to reinforce and worsen panic disorder over time
  rather than resolving it.
- **Underestimating how treatable panic disorder is** — with
  appropriate therapy (often CBT-based) and, for some, medication,
  panic disorder responds well to treatment.

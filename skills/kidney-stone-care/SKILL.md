# Kidney Stone Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Kidney stones are hard deposits formed from minerals and salts inside
the kidneys. Small stones may pass unnoticed; larger stones can cause
severe pain as they move through the urinary tract.

## Common symptoms

Severe pain in the side/back below the ribs, often radiating to the
lower abdomen or groin, pain with urination, blood in urine, nausea and
vomiting, and — with infection — fever.

## General self-care measures

- Drink plenty of water to help small stones pass and to help prevent
  future stones.
- Take prescribed pain relief for symptomatic episodes.
- Follow dietary guidance from your care team, which depends on the
  specific type of stone (not all kidney stones have the same dietary
  cause).
- Strain urine if advised, to catch a passed stone for analysis, which
  helps guide future prevention.

## When to seek medical attention

- Severe pain that isn't controlled by over-the-counter pain relief.
- Fever or chills alongside stone symptoms — can indicate infection,
  which is a more urgent situation; see [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Inability to urinate, or persistent vomiting preventing fluid intake.
- A stone that doesn't pass within the expected timeframe your care
  team advises.

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming all kidney stones are caused by the same dietary factor** —
  different stone types (calcium, uric acid, and others) have different
  causes and prevention strategies; stone analysis after passing one
  helps target the right prevention approach.
- **Delaying care for fever with stone symptoms** — a stone combined
  with infection and urinary obstruction is a more urgent situation
  than an uncomplicated stone.
- **Not increasing fluid intake for prevention after a first stone** —
  kidney stones are prone to recurrence; hydration and other
  prevention measures matter for long-term risk, not just during an
  active episode.

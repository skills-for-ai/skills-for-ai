# Endometriosis Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Endometriosis is a condition where tissue similar to the lining of the
uterus grows outside it, commonly causing pelvic pain and, for some,
fertility difficulties.

## Common symptoms

Pelvic pain (often worse around menstruation), painful periods, pain
during or after sex, digestive symptoms (bloating, pain with bowel
movements), fatigue, and difficulty conceiving.

## General self-care measures

- Track symptoms (pain patterns, cycle timing) to share with your care
  team — this helps guide diagnosis and treatment.
- Use pain management strategies as advised (heat, prescribed pain
  relief, hormonal treatment if appropriate).
- Consider dietary and lifestyle adjustments some people find helpful,
  alongside — not instead of — medical treatment.
- Seek support for the emotional impact of chronic pain and, for some,
  fertility concerns.

## When to seek medical attention

- Pelvic pain that's severe, persistent, or worsening.
- Pain significantly affecting daily life, work, or relationships.
- Difficulty conceiving after trying for the recommended timeframe (see
  [[fertility-assessment]]).
- Sudden, severe abdominal pain — see [[urgent-emergency-care]] to
  rule out an acute complication.

## Common pitfalls

- **Normalizing severe period pain as "just bad periods"** —
  endometriosis is often significantly delayed in diagnosis because
  symptoms are dismissed as normal; persistent severe pain warrants
  evaluation.
- **Assuming treatment is purely about fertility** — many people seek
  care primarily for pain management, independent of fertility plans;
  both are valid reasons for treatment.
- **Stopping hormonal treatment without a plan** — symptom control
  often depends on consistent treatment; changes should be discussed
  with the care team rather than made unilaterally.

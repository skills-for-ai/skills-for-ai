# Vision, Mission, Values

Vision, mission, and values are three distinct components of a company's
strategic plan, representing its purpose, direction, and guiding
principles respectively — commonly confused with each other, but each
answers a genuinely different question.

## The three, distinguished

- **Vision** — the company's overarching aspiration: what it hopes to
  achieve in the long term. Answers "where are we going?" A good vision
  statement is inspiring, future-oriented, and succinct, giving employees
  and stakeholders a clear sense of direction and purpose.
- **Mission** — the company's reason for being: what it does, who it
  serves, and how. Answers "why do we exist, today?" A good mission
  statement is customer-focused, specific, and action-oriented, explaining
  the value the company adds and what differentiates it from competitors.
- **Values** — the company's guiding principles: its beliefs and
  priorities. Answers "how do we behave?" A good set of values is clear,
  concise, and meaningful — representing genuine highest aspirations, and
  providing a real framework for decisions and behavior, not just a wall
  poster.

## Why the distinction matters in practice

A vision without a mission is aspirational with no grounding in present
reality; a mission without a vision is operationally clear but has no
long-term direction; values without either are a list of nice words with
nothing concrete to apply them to. Together, the three give a company a
complete roadmap: aspiration (vision), present purpose (mission), and
behavioral guardrails (values) — each doing a job the other two can't.

## What they're used for

Guiding decision-making, inspiring employees, and communicating the
company's purpose and priorities to external stakeholders — a
consistent, credible vision/mission/values set differentiates a company
from competitors and gives it a foundation strategic and tactical
decisions can actually be checked against.

## Common pitfalls

- **A vision statement that's actually a mission statement** (or vice
  versa) — a common, easy confusion; check which question the statement
  actually answers ("where are we going" vs. "why do we exist today").
- **Values that are generic and interchangeable with any other company's**
  — "integrity, excellence, teamwork" says nothing distinctive; genuinely
  useful values are specific enough to actually guide a hard decision.
- **Vision/mission/values crafted once and never referenced again** — if
  no actual decision gets checked against them, they're decoration, not
  strategy.
- **No connection to trackable execution** — a vision with nothing
  operationalizing it (no North Star, no OKRs) stays aspirational and
  never actually shapes day-to-day work.

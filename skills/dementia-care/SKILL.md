# Dementia Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Dementia is an umbrella term for a group of conditions causing
progressive decline in memory, thinking, and daily functioning —
Alzheimer's disease is the most common cause, but there are several
others (vascular dementia, Lewy body dementia, and more).

## Common symptoms

Progressive memory loss, difficulty with familiar tasks, disorientation
to time or place, language and communication difficulties, poor
judgment, and personality or behaviour changes — see
[[alzheimers-disease-care]] for the specific most-common cause.

## General self-care and caregiving measures

- Maintain consistent routines and a safe, familiar environment.
- Use clear, simple communication and allow extra time for responses.
- Support remaining abilities and independence rather than taking over
  every task.
- Plan for safety (wandering risk, medication management, home
  hazards) proactively.
- Caregivers should seek support for themselves — caregiver stress is
  common and affects care quality.

## When to seek medical attention

- New memory or cognitive concerns, for proper diagnosis rather than
  assuming it's "just aging."
- A sudden change in cognition or alertness — may indicate a separate,
  treatable acute cause; see [[confusion-assessment-method]].
- Safety incidents (wandering, falls, medication errors).
- Significant behavioural changes or distress.

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming memory problems are always "just normal aging"** —
  significant, progressive memory decline warrants evaluation rather
  than automatic dismissal.
- **Missing a treatable acute cause behind sudden confusion** —
  delirium from infection, dehydration, or medication can look like
  worsening dementia but is a distinct, often reversible, condition.
- **Neglecting caregiver wellbeing** — caregiver burnout is a real risk
  that affects both the caregiver's health and the quality of care
  provided.

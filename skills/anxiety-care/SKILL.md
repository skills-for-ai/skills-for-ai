# Anxiety Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Anxiety is a normal response to stress, but when it's excessive,
persistent, or disproportionate to the situation, it can become an
anxiety disorder that interferes with daily life.

## Common symptoms

Excessive worry, restlessness, muscle tension, difficulty concentrating,
irritability, sleep disturbance, and physical symptoms such as rapid
heartbeat, sweating, or shortness of breath.

## General self-care measures

- Practice relaxation techniques (breathing exercises, mindfulness,
  progressive muscle relaxation).
- Maintain regular sleep, exercise, and limit caffeine/alcohol, which
  can worsen anxiety symptoms.
- Challenge unhelpful thought patterns, ideally with support from a
  therapist trained in approaches like CBT.
- Build a support network and talk about what you're experiencing.

## When to seek medical attention

- Anxiety significantly interfering with work, relationships, or daily
  functioning.
- Panic attacks (see [[panic-care]]) or physical symptoms severe
  enough to be mistaken for a medical emergency.
- Thoughts of self-harm — seek immediate help; see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]].

## Common pitfalls

- **Avoiding anxiety-provoking situations entirely** — avoidance
  typically reinforces anxiety over time rather than resolving it;
  gradual, supported exposure is often part of effective treatment.
- **Treating physical symptoms as purely medical without considering
  anxiety** — anxiety can produce very real physical symptoms; both
  possibilities are worth discussing with a doctor.
- **Waiting until anxiety is severe before seeking help** — earlier
  intervention is generally easier and more effective than waiting for
  a crisis point.

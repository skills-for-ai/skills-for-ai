# Sore Throat Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

A sore throat is discomfort, pain, or irritation in the throat, most
commonly caused by a viral infection, though bacterial infections
(like strep throat) and other causes (allergies, dry air, acid reflux)
are also common.

## Common symptoms

Throat pain or scratchiness, pain worsening with swallowing, redness in
the throat, and sometimes swollen glands, fever, or a hoarse voice.

## General self-care measures

- Stay hydrated and consider warm liquids (tea, broth) for comfort.
- Gargle with warm salt water for symptom relief.
- Use throat lozenges or over-the-counter pain relief as appropriate.
- Use a humidifier if dry air seems to be contributing.
- Rest your voice if hoarseness is also present.

## When to seek medical attention

- Severe throat pain, especially with difficulty swallowing or
  breathing — see [[urgent-emergency-care]] for severe or rapidly
  worsening symptoms.
- Sore throat with high fever, swollen glands, and white patches on the
  tonsils (possible strep throat, see [[streptococcus-care]],
  [[tonsillitis-care]]), which may need antibiotic treatment.
- Symptoms lasting more than a week without improvement.
- A sore throat accompanied by a rash, joint pain, or unusual fatigue.

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming every sore throat is viral and self-limiting** — bacterial
  causes like strep throat need antibiotic treatment and can have
  complications if untreated; symptoms suggestive of strep (sudden
  onset, fever, white patches, no cough) warrant testing.
- **Requesting antibiotics for a clearly viral sore throat** —
  antibiotics don't help viral infections and contribute to antibiotic
  resistance when used unnecessarily.
- **Ignoring difficulty breathing or swallowing** — these are red-flag
  symptoms that go beyond typical sore throat and need urgent
  evaluation.

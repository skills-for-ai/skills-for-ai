# TEAM FOCUS Skill

TEAM FOCUS is a framework developed by McKinsey & Company combining two
complementary halves: **TEAM** addresses interpersonal team effectiveness;
**FOCUS** addresses the analytical structure of the problem-solving
process itself. Applying only one half addresses just part of what makes
a team's work actually succeed.

## TEAM (interpersonal)

- **Talk** — establish genuinely effective communication channels; this
  is foundational, not a nice-to-have, since every other element depends
  on information actually flowing.
- **Evaluate** — assess performance and adapt accordingly, rather than
  running on assumption about how things are going.
- **Assist** — help each other; strategically leveraging each person's
  unique capabilities is a defining trait of genuinely high-performing
  ("special forces") teams.
- **Motivate** — pay close attention to what actually drives individual
  team members — generic motivation tactics matter less than
  understanding each person's real drivers.

## FOCUS (analytical)

- **Frame** — before starting, identify the key question being studied,
  draw issue trees exploring potential lines of investigation, and
  develop hypotheses to test — framing well up front prevents wasted
  effort investigating the wrong question.
- **Organize** — a necessary, if unglamorous, step preparing the team for
  efficient problem-solving, organized around the content hypotheses with
  the end goal in mind.
- **Collect** — gather relevant data, deliberately avoiding
  over-collection of data that won't actually contribute to answering the
  key question.
- **Understand** — evaluate collected data for what it actually
  contributes to proving or disproving the hypotheses — the discipline
  of repeatedly asking "so what?" rather than just accumulating findings.
- **Synthesize** — turn analyzed data into a compelling narrative; this is
  where the well-known "pyramid principle" (leading with the conclusion,
  then supporting detail) shapes how a report or presentation is
  structured.

## Why both halves matter together

A team with excellent interpersonal dynamics (TEAM) but no analytical
discipline (FOCUS) can collaborate wonderfully while solving the wrong
problem, or drowning in irrelevant data. A team with rigorous analytical
process but poor interpersonal dynamics burns out or fractures before the
analysis can be completed well. TEAM FOCUS treats both as necessary,
not as a choice between them.

## Common pitfalls

- **Skipping Frame and jumping straight to Collect** — gathering data
  before the key question and hypotheses are actually defined risks
  collecting the wrong data, or far more than actually needed.
- **Treating Understand as just "presenting the data"** — the "so what?"
  discipline is what turns raw analysis into an actual answer to the
  framed question; skipping it leaves conclusions implicit rather than
  stated.
- **Ignoring the TEAM half entirely** — a purely analytical approach to
  FOCUS without attention to Talk/Evaluate/Assist/Motivate risks a
  technically sound analysis delivered by a team that's burned out or
  miscommunicating along the way.
- **Synthesizing without the pyramid principle** — burying the actual
  conclusion under supporting detail, rather than leading with it, makes
  even well-supported findings harder for an audience to act on.

# Rosacea Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Rosacea is a chronic skin condition causing facial redness and, for
some, visible blood vessels, bumps, or eye irritation, with symptoms
that flare and subside over time.

## Common symptoms

Facial redness (especially cheeks, nose, forehead), visible small blood
vessels, bumps or pimples that can resemble acne, and — for some —
eye irritation (ocular rosacea) or thickened skin (more common on the
nose).

## General self-care measures

- Identify and avoid personal triggers (common ones include sun
  exposure, spicy food, alcohol, heat, and stress, though triggers vary
  by individual).
- Use gentle, fragrance-free skincare products.
- Apply broad-spectrum sunscreen daily, since sun exposure is a common
  trigger.
- Follow prescribed treatment consistently, since rosacea is typically
  a long-term, managed condition rather than one that's "cured."

## When to seek medical attention

- New or worsening facial redness/bumps, for proper diagnosis (rosacea
  can resemble other skin conditions).
- Eye symptoms (dryness, irritation, redness) alongside facial rosacea.
- Thickening skin, particularly on the nose, which may need specific
  treatment.
- Symptoms not responding to current treatment.

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating rosacea like acne** — some rosacea treatments overlap with
  acne treatment, but certain acne products can actually worsen
  rosacea; a proper diagnosis matters before choosing treatment.
- **Ignoring eye symptoms as unrelated** — ocular rosacea is a real,
  distinct manifestation that benefits from its own treatment, not just
  facial skin care.
- **Not identifying personal triggers** — since triggers vary
  significantly between individuals, a generic trigger-avoidance list
  is less effective than tracking one's own specific flare patterns.

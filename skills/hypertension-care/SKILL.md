# Hypertension Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Hypertension (high blood pressure) is a common, usually symptomless
condition where the force of blood against artery walls is
consistently too high, increasing risk of heart disease, stroke, and
kidney problems over time.

## Common symptoms

Hypertension is often called a "silent" condition — most people have no
symptoms at all, which is why regular blood pressure checks matter more
than watching for symptoms. Very high blood pressure can occasionally
cause headaches, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds.

## General self-care measures

- Take prescribed medication consistently, even when feeling well.
- Reduce salt intake, maintain a balanced diet, and stay physically
  active as advised.
- Limit alcohol and avoid smoking.
- Monitor blood pressure at home if recommended, and keep a record to
  share with your care team.
- Manage stress and maintain regular sleep.

## When to seek medical attention

- A blood pressure reading far above your usual range, especially with
  symptoms like severe headache, chest pain, or vision changes —
  this can indicate a hypertensive emergency; see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- New or worsening symptoms while on blood pressure medication.
- Missed doses of medication for an extended period.

## Common pitfalls

- **Stopping medication once blood pressure normalizes** — controlled
  blood pressure usually reflects the medication working, not that
  treatment is no longer needed; stopping abruptly can cause a rebound.
- **Relying on home readings taken inconsistently** (different times of
  day, after exercise or caffeine) — consistent conditions give more
  meaningful trends.
- **Treating hypertension as low-priority because it's symptomless** —
  its health impact accumulates silently over years regardless of how
  the person feels day to day.

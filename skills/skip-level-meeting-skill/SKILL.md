# Skip-Level Meeting Skill

A skip-level meeting is a leader meeting with employees who are *not*
their direct reports — one or more levels down in the organization,
deliberately skipping the intermediate level in the chain of command.

## Purpose

Creates an open, transparent communication channel between higher-level
management and front-line employees who otherwise rarely interact
directly with senior leadership — building trust, increasing engagement,
and promoting a sense of organizational community. It also gives leaders
direct insight into the real challenges front-line employees face and
ideas for improving process, unfiltered by however many management
layers sit between them.

## Key aspects

- **Preparation** — review the employees' work, job descriptions,
  performance context, and relevant metrics beforehand, and come with a
  concrete agenda of specific topics rather than an open-ended,
  unstructured conversation.
- **Focus on listening** — the leader's job here is primarily to listen:
  gathering concerns, ideas, questions, and feedback rather than
  dominating the conversation with their own agenda or opinions.
- **Action and follow-up** — genuinely follow up on concerns or ideas
  raised, and communicate back any resulting changes so employees see
  their input was actually taken seriously, not just heard politely.

## The one step easy to skip: closing the loop with the skipped manager

A skip-level meeting, by design, bypasses the employee's direct manager
— which means that manager needs a deliberate follow-up from the leader
afterward, both to maintain trust with that manager and to avoid
undermining their authority. Skipping this step is a common, avoidable
way a skip-level meeting damages the very management layer it passed
over, even when the meeting itself went well.

## Common pitfalls

- **No agenda, turning into an unfocused chat** — reduces the meeting's
  value and can make it feel like a waste of the attendees' time.
- **The leader doing most of the talking** — defeats the format's
  purpose; the value comes specifically from *listening* to people who
  don't normally get direct leadership airtime.
- **No follow-up with the skipped manager** — see above; this is the
  single most damaging omission, since it can make the bypassed manager
  feel undermined or blindsided by decisions made in a conversation they
  weren't part of.
- **Raising concerns with no visible action afterward** — as with
  [[one-on-one-meeting-skill]], feedback gathered but never acted on
  erodes trust in the format and discourages honest input next time.

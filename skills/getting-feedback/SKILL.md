# Getting Feedback

Getting feedback well means actively seeking it out and receiving it in
a way that lets you actually learn from it — distinct from passively
waiting for feedback to arrive, or reflexively defending against it
when it does. See [[giving-feedback]] for the delivering side of the
same exchange.

## Actively seeking feedback

- **Ask specific questions** rather than a generic "any feedback?" —
  "what's one thing that would have made that presentation clearer?" is
  more likely to get a useful, honest answer than an open-ended prompt.
- **Ask before you need it**, not just after something's gone wrong —
  proactively seeking feedback signals that you want it, which makes
  people more willing to give it honestly.
- **Ask multiple people, not just one** — a single perspective can be
  idiosyncratic; patterns across several people's feedback are more
  reliable than any one data point.

## Receiving feedback well

- **Listen fully before responding** — resist the urge to explain,
  justify, or defend while the other person is still talking; you can
  ask clarifying questions without arguing the point.
- **Separate the content from the delivery** — feedback delivered
  clumsily can still contain something true and useful; don't dismiss
  the substance because you didn't like the tone.
- **Say thank you, even when it stings** — this isn't about pretending
  to feel good about criticism; it's recognizing that someone took a
  real risk to tell you something, and reacting badly discourages them
  (and others) from being honest with you again.
- **Decide deliberately what to act on** — not all feedback is equally
  valid or relevant; take time to evaluate it rather than either
  dismissing it reflexively or overcorrecting on a single data point.

## Common pitfalls

- **Getting defensive in the moment** — even a measured, well-
  intentioned defense in real time tends to shut down further honest
  feedback; better to listen fully first and process afterward.
- **Only seeking feedback from people likely to be positive** — this
  produces reassurance, not useful feedback; genuinely useful feedback
  often requires asking people willing to tell you something you don't
  want to hear.
- **Treating every piece of feedback as equally authoritative** — one
  person's strong opinion isn't the same as a pattern across several
  people; weigh feedback accordingly rather than reacting to the most
  recent or most forceful comment.
- **Asking for feedback but visibly never acting on any of it** — this
  teaches people that giving you feedback doesn't matter, and they'll
  stop bothering.

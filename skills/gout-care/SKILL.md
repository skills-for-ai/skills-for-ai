# Gout Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Gout is a form of inflammatory arthritis caused by a buildup of uric
acid crystals in a joint, causing sudden, severe pain and swelling —
most classically in the big toe, though it can affect other joints.

## Common symptoms

Sudden, intense joint pain (often starting at night), swelling, redness,
and warmth in the affected joint, with the skin sometimes appearing
shiny and tender even to light touch.

## General self-care measures

- Rest the affected joint and elevate it if possible during a flare.
- Apply a cold compress for pain and swelling relief.
- Take prescribed anti-inflammatory medication for a flare as directed.
- Follow dietary guidance to reduce future flares (moderating alcohol,
  and foods high in purines, as advised by your care team).
- Stay well hydrated.

## When to seek medical attention

- A first suspected gout attack, to confirm diagnosis and rule out
  other causes (like joint infection).
- Fever alongside a hot, swollen joint — can indicate joint infection,
  a different and more urgent condition; see [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Frequent gout attacks, which may need ongoing preventive (not just
  flare) treatment.
- Gout affecting multiple joints or not responding to usual treatment.

## Common pitfalls

- **Self-diagnosing every joint flare as gout** — a hot, swollen joint
  can also indicate infection or another form of arthritis; especially
  for a first episode, proper evaluation matters.
- **Only treating flares without addressing long-term uric acid
  levels** — frequent flares often warrant preventive, ongoing
  treatment to lower uric acid, not just repeated flare management.
- **Stopping preventive medication once flares stop** — preventive
  treatment for gout is often long-term; stopping it can lead to flares
  returning.

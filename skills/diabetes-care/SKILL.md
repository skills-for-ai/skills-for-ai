# Diabetes Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Diabetes is a group of conditions in which blood glucose (sugar) levels
are too high, either because the body doesn't produce enough insulin
(type 1), doesn't use insulin effectively (type 2), or as a temporary
condition during pregnancy (gestational diabetes).

## Common symptoms

Increased thirst and urination, unexplained fatigue, blurred vision,
slow-healing cuts or wounds, and unexplained weight loss (more typical
of type 1).

## General self-care measures

- Monitor blood glucose as advised by your care team.
- Follow prescribed medication or insulin regimens exactly as directed.
- Maintain a balanced diet and regular physical activity, adjusted to
  your specific health needs.
- Check feet regularly for cuts, sores, or changes in sensation (see
  [[diabetes-podiatry-assessment]]).
- Attend regular reviews — diabetes management is typically an ongoing,
  periodically reassessed process (see [[diabetes-assessment]]).

## When to seek medical attention

- Signs of very high blood glucose (extreme thirst, confusion, rapid
  breathing) or very low blood glucose (shaking, sweating, confusion,
  loss of consciousness).
- A foot wound that isn't healing or shows signs of infection.
- Any symptoms of diabetic ketoacidosis (nausea, vomiting, abdominal
  pain, fruity-smelling breath) — this is a medical emergency; see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]].

## Common pitfalls

- **Skipping regular foot checks** — nerve damage can mean injuries go
  unnoticed until they're serious.
- **Adjusting medication or insulin doses without medical guidance** —
  even well-intentioned self-adjustment can be dangerous.
- **Treating a single good or bad reading as the whole picture** —
  diabetes management is judged over trends and patterns, not one
  reading in isolation.

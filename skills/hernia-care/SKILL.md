# Hernia Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

A hernia occurs when an internal organ or tissue pushes through a weak
spot in the surrounding muscle or connective tissue, most commonly in
the abdomen or groin.

## Common symptoms

A visible or palpable bulge (often more noticeable when standing,
coughing, or straining), discomfort or aching at the site, and a
feeling of heaviness or pressure. Some hernias cause no symptoms
initially.

## General self-care measures

- Avoid heavy lifting or straining that worsens the bulge or
  discomfort.
- Maintain a healthy weight and manage chronic cough or constipation,
  which can put pressure on the area.
- Wear any support garment recommended by your care team if surgery
  isn't immediately planned.
- Discuss with your care team whether monitoring or surgical repair is
  the right approach for your specific hernia.

## When to seek medical attention

- Any new or suspected hernia, for proper diagnosis and to discuss
  management.
- **Seek emergency care immediately** if a hernia becomes suddenly
  painful, firm, discolored, or can't be pushed back in, especially
  with nausea or vomiting — this can indicate a strangulated hernia, a
  surgical emergency; see [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- A hernia that's increasing in size or becoming more symptomatic.

## Common pitfalls

- **Ignoring a painless hernia indefinitely** — even a symptom-free
  hernia is worth medical evaluation, since it can change over time and
  complications can develop.
- **Missing signs of a strangulated hernia** — sudden severe pain,
  firmness, and discoloration at the hernia site are surgical emergency
  signs, not "worse than usual" ordinary hernia discomfort.
- **Continuing heavy lifting or straining activities** that worsen a
  known hernia, rather than modifying activity until it's properly
  addressed.

# Malaria Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Malaria is a serious, potentially life-threatening disease caused by
parasites transmitted through the bite of infected mosquitoes, common
in many tropical and subtropical regions.

## Common symptoms

Fever, chills, headache, muscle aches, fatigue, nausea, and vomiting —
often occurring in cycles. Symptoms can appear anywhere from about a
week to several months after exposure, depending on the malaria
species.

## General self-care and prevention measures

- Take antimalarial prophylaxis medication as prescribed if travelling
  to a malaria-risk area, starting and continuing exactly as directed
  before, during, and after travel.
- Use mosquito prevention measures (repellent, bed nets, appropriate
  clothing, especially at dawn/dusk when relevant mosquitoes are most
  active).
- Complete the full course of any prescribed antimalarial treatment if
  diagnosed, exactly as directed.
- Seek pre-travel advice well before visiting a malaria-risk region, as
  some prophylaxis regimens need to start before departure.

## When to seek medical attention

- Fever developing during or after travel to a malaria-risk area —
  this needs prompt medical evaluation and should be mentioned
  explicitly to the clinician, since malaria can be missed if travel
  history isn't flagged.
- Any fever within several months of travel to a risk area, even if
  prophylaxis was taken (no prophylaxis is 100% effective).
- Severe symptoms (confusion, difficulty breathing, seizures) — this
  can indicate severe malaria, a medical emergency; see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]].

## Common pitfalls

- **Not mentioning recent travel history to a clinician** — malaria can
  be missed or diagnosed late if the treating doctor doesn't know about
  relevant travel; always volunteer this information.
- **Assuming prophylaxis provides complete protection** — no
  antimalarial regimen is 100% effective; fever after travel to a
  risk area still needs evaluation regardless of prophylaxis taken.
- **Stopping prophylaxis early after returning home** — some regimens
  need to continue for a period after leaving the risk area to remain
  effective.

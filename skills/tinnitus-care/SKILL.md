# Tinnitus Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Tinnitus is the perception of sound (ringing, buzzing, hissing) without
an external source. It's a symptom, not a disease itself, with many
possible underlying causes, and can range from mild and occasional to
persistent and distressing.

## Common symptoms

Ringing, buzzing, hissing, or humming sounds perceived in one or both
ears, which may be constant or intermittent, and may be more noticeable
in quiet environments.

## General self-care measures

- Protect your hearing from loud noise going forward, since noise
  exposure is a common contributing factor.
- Use background sound (white noise, soft music) to make tinnitus less
  noticeable, especially when trying to sleep.
- Manage stress and anxiety, which can worsen the perceived severity of
  tinnitus even without changing the underlying sound itself.
- Discuss with your care team whether any current medications might be
  contributing, since some medications can cause or worsen tinnitus.

## When to seek medical attention

- New tinnitus, to identify any underlying cause (earwax buildup,
  hearing loss, medication side effect, and others).
- Tinnitus in only one ear, or accompanied by hearing loss, dizziness,
  or facial numbness — warrants prompt evaluation.
- Tinnitus significantly affecting sleep, concentration, or mental
  health.
- Sudden-onset tinnitus, especially with sudden hearing loss — this can
  need urgent evaluation.

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming tinnitus always has a serious underlying cause** — it
  often doesn't, but evaluation still matters to rule out treatable
  causes and to access strategies for managing it.
- **Focusing entirely on "curing" the sound rather than managing its
  impact** — for many people, tinnitus management focuses on reducing
  distress and improving coping (sound therapy, stress management)
  since the perception itself often can't be eliminated entirely.
- **Ignoring one-sided tinnitus or accompanying symptoms** — these
  patterns warrant more thorough evaluation than typical bilateral,
  noise-related tinnitus.

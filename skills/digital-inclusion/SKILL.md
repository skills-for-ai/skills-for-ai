# Digital Inclusion

Digital inclusion is the concept of ensuring that all individuals and
communities have access to, and can effectively use, digital
technologies — the internet and digital devices — to participate in the
digital world.

## Dimensions

- **Connectivity** — provide affordable and reliable internet access to
  all individuals, regardless of socioeconomic background or geographic
  location, addressing infrastructure, broadband availability, and
  affordability.
- **Digital skills and literacy** — equip individuals with the skills
  and knowledge to effectively navigate and use digital technologies:
  digital literacy, training programs, and empowerment to use digital
  tools for communication, information access, and online services.
- **Accessibility** — address barriers preventing individuals with
  disabilities from accessing and using digital technologies: accessible
  design principles, assistive technologies, and compatibility across
  devices and platforms (see [[web-content-accessibility-guidelines]],
  [[accessibility-testing]] for the concrete standards and verification
  this dimension is built on).
- **Content and services** — promote relevant, culturally appropriate
  digital content and services, so individuals can find information,
  engage online, and access digital resources meeting their needs.
- **Digital citizenship and safety** — promote responsible, safe use of
  digital technologies: online privacy, cybersecurity, digital rights,
  and ethical behavior online.

## Why this is broader than accessibility alone

Accessibility (making digital content usable by people with
disabilities) is one dimension of digital inclusion, not the whole of
it — a fully accessible website is still not digitally inclusive for
someone with no reliable internet connection, no digital literacy
training, or content that isn't available in their language or cultural
context. Digital inclusion asks the question at the level of a whole
population's genuine ability to participate, not just one interface's
compliance with one standard.

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating digital inclusion as synonymous with accessibility** —
  addressing only the accessibility dimension while ignoring
  connectivity, literacy, or cultural relevance leaves large populations
  still excluded.
- **Assuming universal device/connection assumptions** — designing
  digital services assuming reliable broadband and modern devices
  excludes people constrained by cost, infrastructure, or older
  hardware.
- **Providing content without addressing literacy or cultural fit** —
  digital services available in only one language or cultural framing
  aren't genuinely accessible to a diverse population, even if
  technically reachable.
- **No attention to digital safety and rights** — expanding digital
  access without also addressing privacy, security, and digital-rights
  education can expose newly-included populations to real harm.

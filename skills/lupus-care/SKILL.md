# Lupus Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Lupus (systemic lupus erythematosus) is an autoimmune disease where the
immune system attacks the body's own tissues, potentially affecting
skin, joints, kidneys, and other organs, with symptoms that flare and
subside over time.

## Common symptoms

Fatigue, joint pain and swelling, a characteristic facial rash (often
across the cheeks and nose), skin sensitivity to sunlight, fever, and
— depending on which organs are affected — a wide range of other
possible symptoms.

## General self-care measures

- Take prescribed medication consistently, even during symptom-free
  periods — lupus is typically managed as an ongoing condition, not
  just treated during flares.
- Protect skin from sun exposure, a common flare trigger.
- Get adequate rest and pace activity to manage fatigue.
- Attend regular monitoring, since lupus can affect organs like the
  kidneys without obvious symptoms.

## When to seek medical attention

- New or worsening symptoms, especially involving new organ systems.
- Signs of a significant flare (increased fatigue, joint pain, rash,
  fever).
- Any signs of kidney involvement (swelling, changes in urination) or
  other serious complications.
- Fever, which in someone on immunosuppressive treatment can indicate
  infection needing prompt evaluation.

## Common pitfalls

- **Stopping medication once feeling well** — lupus is managed
  proactively to prevent flares and organ damage, not just reactively
  during symptomatic periods.
- **Underestimating sun exposure as a trigger** — sunlight can trigger
  flares in many people with lupus; sun protection is a genuine part of
  management, not just cosmetic skin care.
- **Missing silent organ involvement** — some of the most serious
  effects of lupus (particularly on the kidneys) can develop without
  obvious symptoms, which is why regular monitoring matters even when
  feeling well.

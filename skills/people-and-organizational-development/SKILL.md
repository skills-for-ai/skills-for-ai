# People & Organizational Development

"People & OD" (sometimes "People & Culture," or split across HR and a
separate OD function) is the organizational function responsible for the
workforce and how the organization itself is structured and develops —
distinct from [[organizational-development]], which covers specific
OD diagnostic models and frameworks this function is often the one
selecting and applying to a real workplace problem.

## Core responsibility areas

- **Workforce planning** — forecasting the people/skills an organization
  will need against its strategy, and the build/buy/borrow decision (train
  existing staff, hire externally, or contract) for closing an identified
  gap.
- **Talent management** — attraction, selection, onboarding, performance
  management, succession planning, and retention across the employee
  lifecycle — treated as one connected system rather than isolated HR
  processes, since a weakness in one (e.g. poor onboarding) shows up as a
  symptom elsewhere (e.g. early attrition) if not linked.
- **Learning & development (L&D)** — building organizational and
  individual capability, from onboarding through ongoing skill development
  — see [[continuing-professional-development]] for the individual
  practitioner's side of this.
- **Organizational design** — how the organization is structured
  (reporting lines, team boundaries, decision rights) to actually support
  its strategy; a structure inherited from history rather than
  deliberately designed for current strategy is a common, quiet source of
  friction.
- **Culture and engagement** — the lived norms and how people experience
  working there, related to but distinct from stated values (see
  [[ways-of-working]]'s glossary distinction between values,
  norms, and working agreements).

## Where this connects to organizational-development

When a People & OD function encounters a specific diagnosable problem —
low engagement, a failed restructure, unclear decision rights, resistance
to a change initiative — [[organizational-development]] is where the
diagnostic models, evidence strength ratings, and framework selection
guidance live. This skill describes the organizational role and its
ongoing responsibilities; that one describes the analytical toolkit
applied within it.

## Workforce planning in practice

A workforce plan should connect explicitly to business strategy — "we're
expanding into X" implies specific skill/headcount needs, not a generic
headcount increase. The build/buy/borrow framing forces an explicit choice
per identified gap: **build** (develop existing staff — cheaper, slower,
builds loyalty), **buy** (hire externally — faster, more expensive,
integration risk), or **borrow** (contract/temporary — fastest, most
flexible, no lasting capability left behind).

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating talent-lifecycle stages as unconnected processes** — hiring,
  onboarding, performance management, and retention run by different
  people with no shared view mean a systemic problem (e.g. a broken
  onboarding process causing early attrition) never gets connected to its
  actual cause.
- **Organizational design left as an inherited artifact** — a structure
  that made sense two reorganizations ago, never deliberately revisited
  against current strategy, quietly generates coordination friction that
  gets blamed on individuals rather than structure.
- **Culture initiatives disconnected from actual working agreements** —
  a stated value with no corresponding norm or working agreement (see
  [[ways-of-working]]) is decoration, not culture change.
- **No explicit build/buy/borrow decision** for an identified capability
  gap — defaulting to "just hire" without considering whether developing
  existing staff would build more durable capability, or a contractor
  would close the gap faster for a time-bound need.
- **Confusing this function's ongoing remit with a bounded OD
  intervention** — applying one framework once and considering workforce/
  culture work "done," rather than treating it as a continuous
  organizational responsibility.

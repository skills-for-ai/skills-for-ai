# Eczema Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Eczema (atopic dermatitis) is a chronic condition causing dry, itchy,
inflamed skin, often starting in childhood and commonly linked to other
allergic conditions (asthma, hay fever).

## Common symptoms

Dry, itchy skin, red or discolored patches, thickened or scaly skin
from repeated scratching, and — in flares — weeping or crusted patches,
often in skin folds (elbows, knees) but varying by age and individual.

## General self-care measures

- Moisturize regularly and generously, ideally right after bathing,
  using fragrance-free products.
- Identify and avoid personal triggers (certain soaps, fabrics, heat,
  stress, specific allergens).
- Use lukewarm (not hot) water for bathing, and pat skin dry rather
  than rubbing.
- Follow prescribed treatment consistently during flares, and maintain
  a moisturizing routine even between flares to reduce recurrence.
- Keep nails short to reduce skin damage from scratching.

## When to seek medical attention

- Eczema not controlled by regular moisturizing and over-the-counter
  treatment.
- Signs of skin infection (increasing redness, warmth, oozing, pus, or
  fever) — eczema-affected skin is more prone to infection.
- Widespread or severe flares significantly affecting sleep or daily
  life.
- Eczema in an infant not improving with basic care.

## Common pitfalls

- **Only moisturizing during flares** — regular moisturizing between
  flares helps maintain the skin barrier and can reduce how often
  flares occur.
- **Scratching to relieve itching** — this typically worsens skin
  damage and increases infection risk; addressing the itch through
  moisturizing and treatment (rather than scratching) matters for
  long-term skin health.
- **Missing signs of infection on already-inflamed skin** — it can be
  hard to distinguish a flare from an infection; new warmth, oozing, or
  fever on eczema-affected skin warrants evaluation.

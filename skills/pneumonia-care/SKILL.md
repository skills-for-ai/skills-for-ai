# Pneumonia Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs in one or both
lungs, which can fill with fluid, causing breathing difficulty. It can
range from mild to life-threatening, particularly in young children,
older adults, and those with weakened immune systems.

## Common symptoms

Cough (often with phlegm), fever, chills, shortness of breath, chest
pain that worsens with breathing or coughing, and fatigue. Older adults
may present with confusion instead of typical symptoms.

## General self-care measures

- Complete the full course of any prescribed antibiotics (for bacterial
  pneumonia) exactly as directed, even if feeling better before
  finishing.
- Rest and stay well hydrated.
- Use fever-reducing medication as appropriate.
- Follow up as advised — recovery from pneumonia can take weeks, and
  follow-up care confirms resolution.

## When to seek medical attention

- Suspected pneumonia symptoms, especially in young children, older
  adults, or those with underlying health conditions, who should be
  evaluated promptly.
- Difficulty breathing, chest pain, confusion, or bluish lips/face —
  seek emergency care immediately; see [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Fever that persists or worsens despite treatment.
- Symptoms improving then suddenly worsening again.

## Common pitfalls

- **Mistaking pneumonia for a lingering cold or flu** — persistent or
  worsening symptoms, especially with fever and breathing difficulty,
  need evaluation rather than assuming it will resolve like a common
  cold.
- **Missing atypical presentation in older adults** — confusion, rather
  than typical respiratory symptoms, can be the main sign of pneumonia
  in older adults, which is easy to overlook.
- **Stopping antibiotics early once feeling better** — incomplete
  treatment risks relapse and contributes to antibiotic resistance.

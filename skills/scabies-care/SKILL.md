# Scabies Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Scabies is a contagious skin condition caused by tiny mites that burrow
into the skin, causing intense itching. It spreads through prolonged
skin-to-skin contact.

## Common symptoms

Intense itching (often worse at night), a pimple-like rash, and
tiny burrow marks in the skin, commonly between fingers, on wrists,
elbows, and other skin folds.

## General self-care measures

- Use a prescribed scabies treatment (topical cream/lotion applied to
  the whole body as directed) — this requires a prescription and
  correct, complete application, not partial self-treatment.
- Treat all household members and close contacts simultaneously, even
  those without symptoms yet, since the incubation period can be
  several weeks.
- Wash bedding, clothing, and towels used in the days before treatment
  in hot water, or seal unwashable items away for several days (mites
  don't survive long off skin).
- Expect itching to continue for a couple of weeks after successful
  treatment as the skin heals — this doesn't necessarily mean treatment
  failed.

## When to seek medical attention

- Suspected scabies, for prescription treatment (over-the-counter
  options aren't generally effective).
- Itching or rash persisting well beyond a couple of weeks after
  treatment.
- Widespread, crusted skin lesions (a more severe form requiring
  specific treatment, especially in people with weakened immune
  systems).

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating only the symptomatic person** — because of scabies' long
  incubation period, close contacts without symptoms yet can still be
  infested and should be treated simultaneously to prevent reinfection.
- **Assuming continued itching after treatment means treatment
  failed** — itching commonly persists for one to two weeks after
  successful treatment; this alone doesn't indicate treatment failure.
- **Using an unprescribed or incomplete treatment approach** — proper
  scabies treatment needs correct application to the entire body, not
  just visibly affected areas.

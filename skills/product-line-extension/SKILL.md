# Product Line Extension

Product line extension is a growth strategy that adds new products or
variants to an existing product line, targeting the same (or closely
adjacent) existing customer base, rather than changing who the
business sells to.

## Common forms

- **New variants** — different sizes, flavors, colors, or feature tiers
  of an existing product.
- **Adjacent products** — new products that complement an existing one
  and appeal to the same customer base (a razor company adding shaving
  cream).
- **Premium or budget tiers** — extending a product line upmarket or
  downmarket to capture customers with different willingness to pay.

## Product line extension vs. market expansion

Product line extension grows the *offering* for a largely unchanged
existing audience; [[market-expansion]] grows the *audience* for a
largely unchanged existing offering. They address different growth
levers — extension leverages existing customer relationships and brand
trust to sell more; expansion leverages an existing offering to reach
customers not currently served.

## Benefits

Product line extension can capture more revenue from existing
customers, defend against competitors filling gaps in the product
line, and leverage existing brand equity, distribution relationships,
and customer trust more efficiently than launching an entirely new
brand or entering a new market would.

## Common pitfalls

- **Brand dilution** — extending a product line too far from the
  brand's core identity or quality position can confuse customers and
  weaken the original brand's meaning.
- **Cannibalization** — a new variant or tier can simply shift existing
  customers from one product to another (with no net revenue gain)
  rather than capturing genuinely new spend, if not designed carefully.
- **Extending the line based on internal capability rather than
  validated customer demand** — "we can make this" isn't the same as
  "customers want this"; extension decisions benefit from the same
  customer-need validation as an entirely new product would.

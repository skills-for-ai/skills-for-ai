# Mast Cell Activation Syndrome (MCAS) Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Mast Cell Activation Syndrome (MCAS) is a condition where mast cells
(a type of immune cell) release excessive mediators, causing symptoms
across multiple body systems, often triggered by specific factors that
vary between individuals.

## Common symptoms

Symptoms can affect the skin (flushing, hives), digestive system
(nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal pain), cardiovascular system
(lightheadedness, rapid heartbeat), respiratory system (wheezing,
congestion), and nervous system (headache, brain fog) — often
occurring together in episodes.

## General self-care measures

- Identify and avoid personal triggers (which vary widely — certain
  foods, temperature changes, stress, exercise, or medications for
  different individuals).
- Take prescribed medication (often antihistamines or mast-cell
  stabilizers) consistently as directed.
- Keep a symptom and trigger diary to help identify patterns.
- Carry an adrenaline auto-injector if prescribed for severe reaction
  risk.

## When to seek medical attention

- Symptoms significantly affecting daily life or not explained by
  another diagnosis.
- Signs of a severe reaction (difficulty breathing, swelling, sudden
  drop in blood pressure) — this is a medical emergency; see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Difficulty identifying triggers despite tracking symptoms.

## Common pitfalls

- **Attributing multi-system symptoms to separate, unrelated causes** —
  the value of recognizing MCAS is connecting symptoms across systems
  that might otherwise be investigated in isolation.
- **Self-diagnosing from symptom overlap alone** — MCAS diagnostic
  criteria typically require objective evidence (such as tryptase
  levels) correlated with symptomatic episodes, not symptoms alone.
- **Not carrying prescribed emergency medication** — for those at risk
  of severe reactions, this is a safety-critical precaution, not an
  optional extra.

# Tonsillitis Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Tonsillitis is inflammation of the tonsils, usually caused by a viral
infection but sometimes bacterial (including streptococcus — see
[[streptococcus-care]]).

## Common symptoms

Sore throat, difficulty or pain swallowing, red and swollen tonsils
(sometimes with white patches or spots), fever, swollen neck glands,
and bad breath.

## General self-care measures

- Rest and stay well hydrated, favoring soft foods and warm or cold
  liquids for comfort.
- Gargle with warm salt water for symptom relief.
- Use pain/fever-reducing medication as appropriate.
- Complete the full course of antibiotics if prescribed for a bacterial
  cause, even after symptoms improve.

## When to seek medical attention

- Severe throat pain, especially with difficulty swallowing saliva or
  breathing — see [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Symptoms suggestive of a bacterial cause (fever, white patches, no
  cough) that may need antibiotic treatment.
- Recurrent tonsillitis, which may warrant discussing further options,
  including whether tonsil removal is appropriate.
- Muffled voice, drooling, or asymmetric throat swelling (can indicate
  a peritonsillar abscess, a more serious complication needing prompt
  treatment).

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming tonsillitis is always bacterial and needs antibiotics** —
  many cases are viral and resolve without antibiotics; testing (not
  assumption) determines whether antibiotics are appropriate.
- **Ignoring one-sided throat swelling or a muffled voice** — this
  pattern can indicate a peritonsillar abscess, a complication needing
  urgent treatment rather than routine tonsillitis management.
- **Stopping antibiotics early once feeling better** — for a
  confirmed bacterial cause, completing the full course matters to
  avoid relapse and complications.

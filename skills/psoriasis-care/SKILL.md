# Psoriasis Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Psoriasis is a chronic autoimmune condition causing skin cells to build
up rapidly, forming thick, scaly patches. It's a long-term condition
that flares and subsides over time, and can also affect the joints
(psoriatic arthritis) in some people.

## Common symptoms

Raised, red patches with silvery scales (most commonly on elbows,
knees, and scalp), itching or soreness, dry cracked skin that may
bleed, and — for some — joint pain and stiffness.

## General self-care measures

- Moisturize regularly to reduce dryness and scaling.
- Follow prescribed topical or systemic treatment consistently, even
  during periods of improvement, since psoriasis is a chronic condition
  managed on an ongoing basis.
- Identify and manage personal triggers (stress, certain medications,
  skin injury, infections).
- Consider the psychological impact — psoriasis can affect self-esteem
  and mental health, and support for this is a legitimate part of care.

## When to seek medical attention

- New or worsening psoriasis symptoms, or symptoms not controlled by
  current treatment.
- Joint pain or stiffness alongside skin symptoms (possible psoriatic
  arthritis) — see [[rheumatology-assessment]].
- Signs of skin infection (increasing redness, warmth, pus).
- Widespread, rapidly worsening psoriasis, which can occasionally be a
  more urgent presentation.

## Common pitfalls

- **Stopping treatment once skin clears** — psoriasis is a chronic
  condition; treatment is often maintained or adjusted for the
  long term rather than stopped once symptoms improve.
- **Missing joint symptoms as a separate, unrelated issue** —
  psoriatic arthritis can develop in people with psoriasis and needs
  its own evaluation and treatment; joint symptoms shouldn't be
  dismissed as unrelated.
- **Underestimating the psychological impact** — the visible nature of
  psoriasis can significantly affect mental health and quality of life;
  this is a valid part of the condition to discuss with your care team.

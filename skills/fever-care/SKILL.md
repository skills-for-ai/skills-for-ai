# Fever Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Fever is a temporary rise in body temperature, most often a sign the
body's immune system is responding to an infection. It's a symptom, not
a disease in itself.

## Common symptoms

Elevated body temperature, chills or shivering, sweating, headache,
muscle aches, and general malaise. Fever is often accompanied by
symptoms of the underlying cause (cough, sore throat, digestive upset).

## General self-care measures

- Rest and stay hydrated.
- Use fever-reducing medication (appropriate for age and health status)
  if the fever is causing significant discomfort, following label or
  medical guidance.
- Dress in light clothing and keep the room at a comfortable
  temperature — avoid over-bundling, which can worsen discomfort.
- Monitor temperature and associated symptoms over time.

## When to seek medical attention

- Fever in an infant under 3 months (any fever in this age group
  generally needs prompt medical evaluation).
- Very high fever, or fever lasting more than a few days.
- Fever with a stiff neck, severe headache, rash, difficulty breathing,
  confusion, or persistent vomiting — see [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Fever in someone with a weakened immune system or significant
  underlying health condition.

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating fever as inherently dangerous rather than a normal immune
  response** — the fever itself is rarely harmful at typical levels; the
  underlying cause and associated symptoms matter more than the number
  on the thermometer.
- **Over-bundling to "sweat out" a fever** — this can raise body
  temperature further and cause discomfort rather than helping.
- **Underestimating fever in a young infant** — infants under 3 months
  can deteriorate quickly from infections that would be minor in an
  older child or adult; a fever at this age generally warrants prompt
  evaluation.

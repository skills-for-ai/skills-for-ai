# Mental Health Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Mental health care covers supporting emotional, psychological, and
social wellbeing, and addressing mental health conditions when they
arise — as important to overall health as physical health care.

## General principles

Mental health exists on a spectrum and can fluctuate over time; having
a mental health condition doesn't mean someone is "broken," and seeking
help is a sign of self-awareness, not weakness. Many conditions
(anxiety, depression, and others) are common and treatable.

## General self-care measures

- Maintain basic wellbeing habits: sleep, nutrition, physical activity,
  and social connection.
- Build coping strategies for stress (relaxation techniques, journaling,
  talking to someone trusted).
- Seek professional support proactively rather than only in crisis.
- Reduce stigma by talking openly about mental health where it's safe
  to do so.

## When to seek professional help

- Persistent changes in mood, thinking, or behaviour affecting daily
  life.
- Difficulty coping with a stressful life event.
- Any thoughts of self-harm or harming others — seek immediate help;
  see [[urgent-emergency-care]] or a crisis line.
- Substance use as a coping mechanism.

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating mental health support as only for severe crises** —
  earlier, proactive support is generally more effective than waiting
  until symptoms are severe.
- **Assuming one approach (medication, therapy, lifestyle change) works
  for everyone** — effective treatment is often individualized and may
  take some adjustment to find what works.
- **Underestimating the impact of physical health on mental health** —
  sleep, nutrition, and physical activity materially affect mental
  wellbeing and are often a meaningful part of treatment, not separate
  from it.

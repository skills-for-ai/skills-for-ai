# Alzheimer's Disease Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Alzheimer's disease is the most common cause of dementia — a
progressive brain condition causing memory loss and decline in
thinking, reasoning, and communication abilities over time.

## Common symptoms

Progressive memory loss (especially recent events), difficulty with
familiar tasks, confusion about time or place, difficulty following
conversations, misplacing items, poor judgment, and personality or mood
changes.

## General self-care and caregiving measures

- Maintain routines and a familiar, safe environment.
- Use memory aids (calendars, labeled items, reminder notes).
- Encourage social engagement and gentle mental and physical activity.
- Ensure medication is taken correctly, with support if needed.
- Caregivers should seek their own support — caregiving for Alzheimer's
  is demanding and caregiver burnout is common and real.

## When to seek medical attention

- A sudden, rapid change in cognition or behaviour (may indicate an
  unrelated acute cause, such as infection or delirium — see
  [[confusion-assessment-method]]).
- Wandering, getting lost, or safety incidents at home.
- Signs of depression, agitation, or significant behavioural change.
- See [[urgent-emergency-care]] for any sudden severe symptom.

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming all confusion in dementia is "just the disease"** —
  sudden changes can signal a separate, treatable acute cause (infection,
  medication side effect, dehydration) that needs its own evaluation.
- **Underestimating caregiver strain** — caregiver burnout affects care
  quality and the caregiver's own health; support isn't optional
  self-indulgence.
- **Removing independence faster than necessary** — supporting remaining
  abilities, rather than taking over every task, helps maintain dignity
  and function longer.

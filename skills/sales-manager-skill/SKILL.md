# Sales Manager Skill

Sales management owns converting qualified interest into closed revenue —
running the pipeline, coaching the sales team's technique, and forecasting
what will close and when. It picks up where [[marketing-manager-skill]]'s
demand generation leaves off.

## The pipeline and funnel stages

A typical B2B pipeline: **lead → qualified opportunity → proposal/demo →
negotiation → closed-won/closed-lost**. Each stage should have an explicit
**exit criterion** (what has to be true to legitimately move a deal
forward) — without one, pipeline stages become a matter of individual rep
optimism rather than a shared, auditable definition, and forecast accuracy
collapses.

## Qualification frameworks

- **BANT** (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) — a simple, widely-used
  qualification checklist: does the prospect have budget, are we talking
  to someone with real authority to buy, is there a genuine need, and is
  there an actual timeline driving urgency?
- **MEDDIC** (Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision
  process, Identify pain, Champion) — a deeper qualification framework
  common in complex/enterprise sales, forcing explicit answers about who
  the real economic buyer is and who inside the account is actually
  championing the deal, not just who's friendly in meetings.
- **SPIN selling** (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff) — a
  questioning technique for a sales conversation itself, moving a prospect
  from describing their situation to recognizing a problem's real
  implications to articulating the value of solving it, rather than the
  seller asserting the value unprompted.

## Forecasting

A sales forecast is only as good as the pipeline discipline behind it —
commonly categorized as **commit** (high confidence, will close this
period), **best case** (possible with more work), and **pipeline**
(earlier-stage, uncertain). A forecast built from reps' raw optimism
without a manager's independent judgment (informed by stage exit criteria
and deal history) tends to run persistently over-optimistic, a well-known
and largely predictable bias, not a one-off surprise.

## CRM discipline

The pipeline data is only useful if reps log it consistently and honestly
— stage, next steps, close date, key stakeholders. A sales manager's job
includes enforcing this discipline (not just building dashboards from
whatever happens to be entered), since a CRM with stale or inflated data
produces a forecast and pipeline review that's actively misleading rather
than just incomplete.

## Coaching and quota management

- **Quota** — the revenue/deal target a rep is measured against; should be
  set based on territory/market realism, not just divided evenly across a
  team regardless of actual opportunity size in each rep's patch.
- **Deal reviews / pipeline reviews** — a manager's regular, structured
  look at a rep's active deals, focused on coaching the *process* (is the
  qualification real, is the next step concrete) rather than just asking
  "will this close."
- **Win/loss analysis** — systematically reviewing why deals were actually
  won or lost (not just closed) is one of the highest-leverage inputs to
  improving both sales technique and the product/pricing itself, and is
  commonly skipped in practice.

## Common pitfalls

- **Sandbagging or inflating pipeline stages** — a deal parked at
  "proposal sent" for months with no real exit-criteria progress corrupts
  both forecast accuracy and the manager's ability to coach effectively.
- **Quota set without territory/market realism** — an unreachable quota
  demoralizes and doesn't actually drive more revenue; it just changes
  which number reps report against.
- **Skipping qualification under pipeline-volume pressure** — chasing deal
  count without genuine BANT/MEDDIC qualification fills the pipeline with
  deals that were never going to close, hiding the real pipeline health.
- **No win/loss analysis** — without it, the same avoidable objections or
  competitive losses repeat without the pattern ever being surfaced to
  product, marketing, or sales training.

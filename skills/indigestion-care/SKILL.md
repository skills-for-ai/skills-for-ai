# Indigestion Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Indigestion (dyspepsia) is discomfort or pain in the upper abdomen,
often related to eating, with causes ranging from diet and lifestyle to
underlying digestive conditions.

## Common symptoms

Upper abdominal discomfort or burning, bloating, feeling overly full
after eating, nausea, and belching. Symptoms are often, but not always,
related to meals.

## General self-care measures

- Eat smaller, more frequent meals rather than large ones.
- Identify and reduce personal trigger foods (often fatty, spicy, or
  acidic foods, caffeine, or alcohol).
- Avoid lying down soon after eating.
- Use over-the-counter antacids for occasional symptoms as directed.
- Manage stress, which can worsen digestive symptoms for some people.

## When to seek medical attention

- Frequent or persistent indigestion, especially if it's new or
  changing in character.
- Difficulty swallowing, unintentional weight loss, or persistent
  vomiting alongside indigestion (red-flag symptoms warranting prompt
  evaluation).
- Indigestion with chest pain, shortness of breath, or pain radiating
  to the arm/jaw — treat as potentially cardiac and seek emergency
  care; see [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Symptoms not responding to over-the-counter treatment.

## Common pitfalls

- **Relying on antacids long-term without medical evaluation** —
  persistent need for antacids can indicate an underlying condition
  (like reflux disease or an ulcer) worth diagnosing rather than only
  symptom-managing indefinitely.
- **Dismissing chest discomfort as "just indigestion"** — cardiac and
  digestive symptoms can overlap, especially in presentations that
  don't fit a classic pattern; when uncertain, treat as potentially
  cardiac.
- **Ignoring red-flag symptoms** (difficulty swallowing, weight loss,
  persistent vomiting) that indicate the cause needs proper
  investigation rather than routine symptom management.

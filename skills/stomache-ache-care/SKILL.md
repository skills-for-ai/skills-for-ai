# Stomach Ache Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

A stomach ache (abdominal pain) has many possible causes, ranging from
minor and self-limiting (indigestion, gas, mild stomach upset) to
conditions needing prompt medical evaluation.

## Common symptoms

Cramping, dull ache, sharp pain, or bloating in the abdomen, sometimes
with nausea, changes in bowel habits, or loss of appetite. Location and
character of pain can give clues to the cause.

## General self-care measures

- Rest and avoid solid food temporarily if nauseated, then reintroduce
  bland foods gradually.
- Stay hydrated with small, frequent sips of fluid.
- Use a heating pad on the abdomen for comfort with mild cramping.
- Avoid foods that seem to trigger or worsen symptoms while
  investigating the cause.

## When to seek medical attention

- Severe, sudden, or worsening abdominal pain.
- Pain localized to one specific area, especially the lower right
  abdomen (possible appendicitis) — see [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Abdominal pain with fever, persistent vomiting, blood in vomit or
  stool, or yellowing of the skin/eyes.
- Pain lasting more than a day or two, or recurring frequently.

## Common pitfalls

- **Dismissing severe or localized pain as "just a stomach ache"** —
  certain patterns (sudden severe pain, pain that moves and settles in
  the lower right abdomen) can indicate a surgical emergency like
  appendicitis and need urgent evaluation.
- **Using pain relief to mask severe pain without seeking evaluation**
  — this can delay diagnosis of a serious underlying cause.
- **Assuming all abdominal pain resolves with rest and time** — while
  many causes are minor, persistent, recurring, or worsening pain
  warrants proper evaluation rather than repeated self-treatment.

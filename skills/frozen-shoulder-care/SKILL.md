# Frozen Shoulder Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis) is a condition causing pain and
progressive stiffness in the shoulder joint, typically developing
gradually over months and often resolving over one to three years,
sometimes with treatment to speed recovery.

## Common symptoms

Shoulder pain and progressively worsening stiffness, typically
progressing through phases: a painful "freezing" phase, a stiff
"frozen" phase with less pain but very limited movement, and a
"thawing" phase with gradual improvement.

## General self-care measures

- Continue gentle movement and stretching within the limits of pain —
  complete immobilization can worsen stiffness.
- Use heat before stretching exercises for comfort.
- Use prescribed pain relief as needed to allow for movement/therapy
  participation.
- Attend physical therapy if recommended — a structured exercise
  program can help through each phase.

## When to seek medical attention

- Shoulder pain and stiffness that persists or worsens over weeks.
- Significant impact on daily activities (dressing, reaching,
  sleeping).
- No improvement despite following a treatment plan.

## Common pitfalls

- **Avoiding all shoulder movement due to pain** — while pain limits
  what's comfortable, complete immobilization tends to worsen stiffness
  over time; guided, gentle movement is generally recommended instead.
- **Expecting rapid resolution** — frozen shoulder often takes many
  months to over a year to resolve, even with appropriate treatment;
  managing expectations matters for sticking with a long-term plan.
- **Assuming all shoulder pain is frozen shoulder** — rotator cuff
  injuries, arthritis, and other shoulder conditions can present
  similarly; a proper evaluation distinguishes them since management
  differs.

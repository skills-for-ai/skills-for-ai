# Mind Map

A mind map is a graphical tool for visually organizing and structuring
ideas and information — starting from a central idea or concept and
branching out to related ideas or subtopics, radiating outward rather
than listed linearly top to bottom.

## Structure

The main idea sits at the center of the diagram; branches radiate out
from it representing subtopics, and each subtopic can be expanded further
with its own sub-branches — a hierarchical, radial structure rather than
a linear outline, which is what makes relationships between ideas visible
at a glance in a way a bulleted list doesn't.

## Common uses

Brainstorming, problem-solving, note-taking, and organizing information —
for both personal use (studying, personal planning) and professional use
(project planning, presentation structuring). It's one of the specific
techniques [[ideation]] lists among its broader idea-generation
toolkit.

## Why it works

- **Better organization** — structures information logically, making it
  easier to understand than an unstructured list.
- **Increased creativity** — the branching, non-linear format encourages
  free association, letting more creative connections emerge than a
  strictly linear format would prompt.
- **Improved memory retention** — visual and spatial relationships help
  the brain retain information more effectively than plain text alone.
- **Enhanced communication** — complex ideas can be conveyed simply and
  concisely through the visual structure.

## How to build one

Start with the central idea in the middle (traditionally circled), then
draw branches out to represent related subtopics, expanding each with
further branches and sub-branches as needed. Color, images, and symbols
add visual distinction between branches and help the map read faster at
a glance — a monochrome, text-only mind map loses some of the visual-
memory benefit the format is specifically good for.

## Common pitfalls

- **Too many levels of nesting** — a mind map that's grown too deep loses
  the at-a-glance clarity that's its main advantage; consider splitting
  an overgrown branch into its own separate map.
- **No visual distinction between branches** — skipping color/icons
  reduces the technique to a radially-arranged text outline, losing the
  memory-retention benefit visual distinctiveness provides.
- **Using a mind map for content that's genuinely linear/sequential** —
  a step-by-step process is usually clearer as a flowchart or numbered
  list; mind maps suit associative, non-sequential idea structures best.

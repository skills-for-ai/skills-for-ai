# Gallstone Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Gallstones are hardened deposits that form in the gallbladder, ranging
from tiny "gravel" to golf-ball-sized stones. Many gallstones cause no
symptoms and are found incidentally; others cause significant pain or
complications.

## Common symptoms

Sudden, intense pain in the upper right abdomen (often after a fatty
meal), pain that may spread to the back or shoulder, nausea and
vomiting, and — with complications — fever or jaundice (yellowing of
skin/eyes).

## General self-care measures

- Follow dietary guidance if recommended (some people find lower-fat
  meals reduce symptom frequency, though this doesn't dissolve existing
  stones).
- Maintain a healthy weight through gradual, steady changes — rapid
  weight loss can actually increase gallstone risk.
- Take prescribed pain relief for symptomatic episodes as directed.
- Discuss treatment options (monitoring vs. surgical removal of the
  gallbladder) with your care team based on symptom frequency and
  severity.

## When to seek medical attention

- Sudden, severe abdominal pain, especially lasting more than a few
  hours.
- Fever, chills, or yellowing of the skin/eyes alongside abdominal pain
  — this can indicate a serious complication (infection or blocked bile
  duct) and needs urgent evaluation; see [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Pain that keeps recurring.

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating symptomatic gallstone attacks as something to just "wait
  out" repeatedly** — recurrent attacks tend to continue or worsen, and
  can lead to complications; recurring episodes warrant a treatment
  discussion.
- **Attempting rapid weight loss to prevent gallstones** — paradoxically,
  rapid weight loss is itself a risk factor for gallstone formation;
  gradual, steady weight change is generally preferable.
- **Dismissing fever or jaundice alongside abdominal pain as unrelated**
  — this combination can indicate a serious complication requiring
  urgent care, not routine follow-up.

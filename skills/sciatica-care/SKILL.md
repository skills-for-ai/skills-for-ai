# Sciatica Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Sciatica is pain caused by irritation or compression of the sciatic
nerve, typically radiating from the lower back down through the hip
and leg, most often caused by a herniated disc or spinal narrowing.

## Common symptoms

Pain radiating from the lower back through the buttock and down one
leg (usually one-sided), which can range from mild ache to sharp,
burning pain, sometimes with numbness, tingling, or weakness in the
leg or foot.

## General self-care measures

- Stay as active as tolerable — prolonged bed rest is generally not
  recommended and can delay recovery.
- Use heat or cold therapy for symptom relief as suits the individual.
- Take over-the-counter pain relief as appropriate, and follow any
  prescribed treatment or physical therapy program.
- Practice good posture and body mechanics, especially when lifting.

## When to seek medical attention

- Sciatica pain that's severe or doesn't improve with self-care over a
  couple of weeks.
- Progressive weakness or numbness in the leg or foot.
- **Seek emergency care immediately** for loss of bladder or bowel
  control, or numbness in the groin/inner thighs alongside back pain —
  this can indicate a rare but serious condition (cauda equina
  syndrome) requiring urgent treatment; see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]].

## Common pitfalls

- **Resting in bed for an extended period** — unlike some other
  injuries, prolonged bed rest for sciatica generally isn't helpful and
  can slow recovery; staying appropriately active tends to help more.
- **Missing red-flag symptoms** (bladder/bowel changes, groin numbness)
  that indicate a rare surgical emergency rather than routine sciatica
  — these need immediate attention, not a wait-and-see approach.
- **Assuming all leg pain radiating from the back is sciatica** — other
  conditions can mimic sciatica; persistent or unusual presentations
  warrant proper evaluation.

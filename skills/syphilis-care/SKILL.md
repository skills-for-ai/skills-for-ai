# Syphilis Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Syphilis is a bacterial sexually transmitted infection that progresses
through stages if untreated. It's curable with antibiotics, especially
when caught early, but untreated syphilis can cause serious long-term
complications.

## Common symptoms

**Primary stage:** a usually painless sore (chancre) at the infection
site. **Secondary stage:** rash (often on palms/soles), fever, swollen
glands, fatigue. **Latent stage:** no symptoms, but the infection
remains present. **Late/tertiary stage** (if untreated for years): can
affect the heart, brain, and other organs.

## General self-care measures

- Complete the full prescribed antibiotic treatment exactly as
  directed.
- Avoid sexual contact until treatment is complete and a doctor
  confirms it's safe to resume.
- Notify sexual partners so they can be tested and treated —
  confidential partner-notification support is often available through
  health services.
- Get tested for other sexually transmitted infections, since
  co-infection is common.

## When to seek medical attention

- Any sore, rash, or symptoms suggestive of an STI, or after a
  potential exposure — testing doesn't require waiting for symptoms,
  since syphilis can be asymptomatic (latent stage).
- Syphilis during pregnancy — needs prompt treatment to prevent
  transmission to the baby, which can be serious.
- Follow-up testing after treatment to confirm the infection has
  cleared.

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming a painless sore isn't worth evaluating** — the primary-
  stage sore is often painless and can be easily missed or dismissed,
  delaying diagnosis and treatment.
- **Believing symptoms disappearing means the infection is gone** —
  syphilis can enter a symptom-free latent stage while still present in
  the body and capable of causing damage later if untreated.
- **Not notifying partners** — untreated partners can be re-infected or
  continue to unknowingly spread the infection.

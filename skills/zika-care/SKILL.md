# Zika Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Zika virus is a mosquito-transmitted infection (also transmissible
sexually and from mother to baby during pregnancy) that's usually mild
in most people, but poses a serious risk of birth defects if a
pregnant person is infected.

## Common symptoms

Mild fever, rash, joint pain, red eyes (conjunctivitis), and headache
— many people infected have mild symptoms or none at all, which is part
of why exposure history matters even without symptoms.

## General self-care and prevention measures

- Use mosquito prevention measures (repellent, protective clothing, bed
  nets) when travelling to or living in a Zika-risk area.
- Pregnant people should discuss travel to Zika-risk areas with a
  doctor beforehand, given the serious risk to fetal development, and
  consider postponing non-essential travel to affected areas.
- Use protection during sex or abstain for a period after travel to a
  risk area, since Zika can be sexually transmitted, following current
  guidance on timing.
- Rest and stay hydrated if experiencing mild symptoms.

## When to seek medical attention

- Any symptoms during or after travel to a Zika-risk area — mention
  the travel history explicitly to the clinician.
- Pregnancy with any possible Zika exposure (travel to a risk area, or
  a partner with possible exposure) — needs prompt discussion with a
  doctor regardless of symptoms.
- Severe symptoms — while rare, these need evaluation; see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]] for any severe or rapidly worsening
  symptoms.

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming no symptoms means no risk to a pregnancy** — Zika
  infection during pregnancy can pose a serious risk to fetal
  development even when the pregnant person has mild or no symptoms;
  exposure history matters as much as symptoms.
- **Not considering sexual transmission** — Zika can be transmitted
  sexually, which is a distinct risk from mosquito exposure and needs
  its own precautions after travel to a risk area.
- **Underestimating the importance of pre-travel advice during
  pregnancy** — planning and risk discussion before travel to an
  affected area is far more effective than addressing exposure after
  the fact.

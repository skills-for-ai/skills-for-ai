# Croup Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Croup is a common childhood respiratory infection causing swelling
around the vocal cords and windpipe, leading to a distinctive barking
cough, most often affecting children under 5.

## Common symptoms

A harsh, "barking" cough, hoarse voice, and a high-pitched squeaking
sound when breathing in (stridor), often worse at night and sometimes
preceded by cold-like symptoms.

## General self-care measures

- Keep the child calm — crying and distress can worsen breathing
  difficulty and stridor.
- Sit the child upright rather than lying down to ease breathing.
- Offer fluids to prevent dehydration, especially if the child is
  reluctant to eat.
- Use a cool-mist humidifier or, for some children, brief exposure to
  cool night air, which some caregivers find helpful (evidence for
  specific home remedies varies; discuss with a doctor).
- Monitor breathing closely, especially overnight when symptoms often
  worsen.

## When to seek medical attention

- Stridor (noisy breathing) at rest, not just with crying or activity.
- Difficulty breathing, blue-tinged lips or skin, or the child appearing
  to struggle for breath — seek emergency care immediately; see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- High fever, drooling, or difficulty swallowing (can indicate a
  different, more serious condition needing urgent evaluation).
- Symptoms not improving, or worsening, over several days.

## Common pitfalls

- **Letting a child become distressed or cry excessively** — this can
  worsen airway narrowing and breathing difficulty; calm reassurance
  matters clinically, not just emotionally.
- **Assuming croup always resolves without medical review** — most
  cases are mild, but any stridor at rest or breathing difficulty
  warrants prompt medical assessment.
- **Confusing croup's barking cough with other causes of noisy
  breathing** — a similar presentation with high fever and drooling can
  indicate a different, more urgent condition; a professional
  evaluation distinguishes them.

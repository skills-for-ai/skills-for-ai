# Attention Deficit Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental
condition affecting attention, impulse control, and/or activity level,
in both children and adults.

## Common symptoms

Difficulty sustaining attention, distractibility, disorganization,
forgetfulness, impulsivity, and — for some — hyperactivity/restlessness.
Presentation varies by age and by ADHD subtype.

## General self-care and management measures

- Follow a prescribed treatment plan (which may include medication,
  therapy, or both) consistently.
- Use external structure — routines, reminders, calendars, and broken-
  down tasks — to support executive function.
- Regular physical activity and sleep support attention and mood
  regulation.
- For children, coordinate consistent approaches between home and
  school.

## When to seek medical attention

- Symptoms significantly affecting daily functioning, relationships,
  work, or school.
- Side effects from medication, or medication that seems ineffective.
- Co-occurring symptoms of anxiety or depression, which are common
  alongside ADHD.

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating ADHD as a discipline or willpower issue** — it's a
  neurodevelopmental condition; approaches that assume it's simply about
  effort tend not to work and can add unhelpful shame.
- **Stopping medication without discussing it with a prescriber** —
  including due to side effects, which can often be managed by
  adjustment rather than discontinuation.
- **Missing common co-occurring conditions** — anxiety, depression, and
  learning difficulties are common alongside ADHD and can be missed if
  attention is focused only on the ADHD symptoms.

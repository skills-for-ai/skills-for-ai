# Laryngitis Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Laryngitis is inflammation of the voice box (larynx), usually caused
by a viral infection, overuse of the voice, or irritants, resulting in
hoarseness or loss of voice.

## Common symptoms

Hoarseness or loss of voice, a dry or sore throat, a tickling or raw
feeling in the throat, and a dry cough. Symptoms are often accompanied
by other cold-like symptoms if caused by a viral infection.

## General self-care measures

- Rest your voice — avoid shouting, whispering (which can strain the
  voice more than speaking softly), and prolonged talking.
- Stay well hydrated and consider a humidifier for dry air.
- Avoid smoking and irritants like secondhand smoke.
- Avoid excessive caffeine and alcohol, which can dry out the throat.

## When to seek medical attention

- Hoarseness lasting more than two to three weeks.
- Difficulty breathing or swallowing alongside voice changes.
- Coughing up blood.
- Hoarseness in someone who smokes heavily or has other risk factors,
  especially if persistent.

## Common pitfalls

- **Whispering to "rest" the voice** — whispering can actually strain
  the vocal cords more than speaking normally at a soft volume; true
  vocal rest (minimal talking) is more effective.
- **Ignoring persistent hoarseness beyond a few weeks** — most
  laryngitis resolves within one to two weeks; hoarseness lasting
  significantly longer warrants evaluation to rule out other causes.
- **Continuing to smoke or use the voice heavily during recovery** —
  both delay healing of the irritated vocal cords.

# Community of Practice (CoP) Skill

A Community of Practice (CoP) is a group of individuals sharing a common
interest, a set of problems or challenges, and a desire to deepen their
knowledge and expertise in a particular area — a term coined by Etienne
Wenger and Jean Lave in *Situated Learning: Legitimate Peripheral
Participation* (1991). See [[practice-management-skill]] for this
concept placed alongside its client-facing "professional practice"
counterpart meaning.

## What makes it a CoP, specifically

CoPs are **informal networks** — found in corporations, government
agencies, nonprofits, and academic institutions — bringing together
people who share a passion for a specific field or practice, typically
from different backgrounds, roles, and experience levels, and crucially
**cutting across formal reporting lines** rather than following them.

## What members do

Learn from each other, share best practices, and collaborate on
projects — members take an active role in both their own learning and
in supporting others' development, through activities like sharing
knowledge, giving feedback, solving problems together, and conducting
research.

## Benefits

Increased knowledge sharing, improved problem-solving, enhanced
innovation, and increased collaboration — plus a less tangible but real
benefit: building a genuine sense of community and a culture of
continuous learning that a purely formal org structure often doesn't
provide on its own.

## Building a successful CoP

- **Clear purpose and scope** — a CoP with no defined focus tends to
  dissolve into a generic social group rather than sustaining genuine
  knowledge-sharing value.
- **A diverse membership** — bringing in people from different
  backgrounds and experience levels, not just one team or seniority
  band.
- **Real opportunities for engagement** — active participation
  mechanisms, not just a passive mailing list or channel nobody actually
  posts in.
- **Ongoing communication and knowledge-sharing support** — sustained
  effort, not a one-time kickoff event.
- **Facilitation** — a leader or coordinator organizing activities,
  moderating discussion, and providing resources; a CoP with no one
  actively tending it tends to fade over time (see
  [[practice-management-skill]]'s discussion of a practice lead role for
  the closely related internal-organization version of this
  responsibility).

## Common pitfalls

- **No clear purpose** — a vague or overly broad scope makes it hard for
  potential members to know whether the CoP is relevant to them.
- **No active facilitation** — a CoP left to run itself with no one
  organizing activities or moderating discussion tends to go quiet.
- **Treating it as a formal reporting structure** — a CoP's value comes
  specifically from being informal and cutting across formal lines;
  imposing hierarchy or mandatory attendance undermines the voluntary,
  passion-driven participation that makes it work.
- **No mechanism for members to actually contribute**, not just consume
  — a CoP where only the facilitator ever shares content isn't really
  functioning as a community.

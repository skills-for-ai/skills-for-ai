# Sepsis Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Sepsis is a life-threatening condition where the body's response to an
infection injures its own tissues and organs. It's a **medical
emergency** requiring immediate treatment — sepsis can progress rapidly
and become fatal without prompt care.

## Common symptoms

Fever or low body temperature, rapid heart rate, rapid breathing,
confusion or disorientation, extreme pain or discomfort, clammy or
sweaty skin, and (in severe cases, septic shock) very low blood
pressure. Symptoms can develop rapidly, sometimes within hours.

## What to do

- **Sepsis is a medical emergency.** If sepsis is suspected — especially
  in someone with a known infection who develops confusion, rapid
  breathing, or extreme illness — seek emergency medical care
  immediately; see [[urgent-emergency-care]]. Do not wait to see if
  symptoms improve.
- Mention explicitly to emergency responders or clinicians if sepsis is
  suspected — naming the possibility helps prompt rapid evaluation and
  treatment, since early treatment significantly improves outcomes.
- Anyone recovering from sepsis should follow up as advised — recovery
  can be prolonged and some effects (fatigue, weakness) can persist for
  weeks or months.

## Who is at higher risk

Infants, older adults, people with chronic illness or weakened immune
systems, and anyone with an untreated or worsening infection are at
higher risk — but sepsis can affect anyone with an infection,
including previously healthy people.

## Common pitfalls

- **Waiting to see if symptoms improve before seeking care** — sepsis
  can progress from early symptoms to life-threatening organ failure
  within hours; time matters more with sepsis than with most
  conditions.
- **Assuming sepsis only follows severe, obvious infections** — sepsis
  can develop from infections that initially seemed minor (a urinary
  tract infection, a skin infection, pneumonia); any worsening
  infection with the symptoms above warrants urgent attention.
- **Not mentioning "sepsis" explicitly when seeking care** — naming the
  concern directly to healthcare providers helps ensure it's evaluated
  and treated as the time-critical emergency it is.

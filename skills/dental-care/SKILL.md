# Dental Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Good oral health involves preventing and managing tooth decay, gum
disease, and other conditions affecting the teeth, gums, and mouth.

## Common issues

Tooth decay (cavities), gum disease (gingivitis and periodontitis),
tooth sensitivity, bad breath, and tooth pain.

## General self-care measures

- Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste and clean between teeth
  daily (floss or interdental brushes).
- Limit sugary and acidic foods and drinks.
- Attend regular dental checkups, even without symptoms — many dental
  problems progress silently.
- Avoid smoking and limit alcohol, both of which increase oral health
  risks.

## When to seek dental attention

- Persistent tooth pain, especially if severe or worsening.
- Swelling of the face, gums, or jaw — can indicate a spreading
  infection.
- Bleeding gums that don't improve with better oral hygiene.
- A knocked-out or broken tooth — timing matters for a knocked-out
  tooth; seek dental care immediately.
- See [[urgent-emergency-care]] for facial swelling with fever or
  difficulty breathing/swallowing.

## Common pitfalls

- **Only seeing a dentist when something hurts** — many dental
  conditions (early decay, gum disease) are painless until advanced;
  regular checkups catch problems earlier and more cheaply.
- **Brushing harder rather than more consistently to improve oral
  health** — excessive brushing force can damage enamel and gums; gentle,
  consistent, thorough brushing matters more than pressure.
- **Ignoring a knocked-out tooth's time-sensitivity** — re-implantation
  chances drop quickly; treat it as a dental emergency requiring
  immediate attention.

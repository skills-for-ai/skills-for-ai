# Futurespective Skill

A futurespective is a group activity exploring and envisioning possible
futures for a team, organization, or project — a forward-thinking
counterpart to the familiar backward-looking retrospective, aimed at
identifying potential opportunities and challenges and preparing for
possible change before it happens rather than reacting once it does.

## The core mechanism

Participants imagine a range of possible future scenarios — commonly
best-case and worst-case outcomes among others — and think through the
factors that could actually lead to each one. Using these imagined
scenarios to inform *current* decision-making and planning is the whole
point: a futurespective is judged by whether it actually changes a
present decision, not by how interesting the imagined scenarios were on
their own.

## Running one

Typically done with a group (a team or department), often with a trained
facilitator, since keeping the group genuinely imaginative and willing to
challenge assumptions about the future is harder to sustain without
outside facilitation. Participants are explicitly encouraged to think
creatively and challenge their own assumptions about how the future will
unfold — a futurespective that only extrapolates the present trend
in a straight line has missed the exercise's actual value.

## Tools commonly used within a futurespective

- **Brainstorming** — generating a wide range of possible future
  scenarios (see [[brainstorming-skill]]).
- **Scenario planning** — structured exploration of distinct, plausible
  future states.
- **SWOT analysis** — applied to each imagined scenario, to assess
  strengths/weaknesses/opportunities/threats specific to that future (see
  [[swot-analysis-skill]]).

## Where it's most valuable

Particularly useful for projects with high uncertainty — new product
development, strategic planning — where the range of plausible futures
is wide enough that planning against only the single most-likely scenario
leaves the team unprepared for a real but less-expected outcome.

## Common pitfalls

- **Only imagining the expected, likely future** — the value of exploring
  best-case *and* worst-case scenarios specifically comes from
  considering outcomes outside the default expectation; a futurespective
  that only refines the already-assumed future misses its point.
- **No connection back to a present decision** — a futurespective that
  produces interesting scenarios but changes nothing about current
  planning hasn't actually delivered its intended value.
- **Running it without facilitation for a team unused to challenging its
  own assumptions** — without a facilitator actively pushing past
  comfortable, expected extrapolations, the exercise tends to converge
  back on the group's existing consensus view of the future.

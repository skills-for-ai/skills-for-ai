# Urinary Tract Infection (UTI) Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

A urinary tract infection (UTI) is a bacterial infection anywhere in
the urinary system, most commonly the bladder (cystitis), causing
uncomfortable urinary symptoms.

## Common symptoms

A burning sensation when urinating, frequent or urgent need to urinate,
cloudy or strong-smelling urine, pelvic or lower abdominal discomfort,
and — if the infection spreads to the kidneys — fever, back pain, and
nausea.

## General self-care measures

- Complete the full prescribed course of antibiotics exactly as
  directed, even after symptoms improve.
- Drink plenty of water to help flush the urinary system.
- Urinate when needed rather than holding it for extended periods.
- Consider urinating after sexual activity, which some people find
  reduces UTI frequency.

## When to seek medical attention

- Symptoms of a UTI — most UTIs need a urine test and antibiotics
  rather than resolving on their own.
- Fever, back or side pain, chills, or nausea alongside urinary
  symptoms — can indicate the infection has spread to the kidneys, a
  more serious infection needing prompt treatment; see
  [[urgent-emergency-care]] if severe.
- Frequent recurrent UTIs, which may need further evaluation.
- UTI symptoms during pregnancy, which need prompt treatment.
- Blood in the urine.

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming symptoms will resolve without treatment** — unlike some
  minor infections, most UTIs benefit from antibiotic treatment and can
  worsen (including spreading to the kidneys) if untreated.
- **Stopping antibiotics early once symptoms improve** — incomplete
  treatment risks relapse and doesn't fully clear the infection.
- **Mistaking kidney infection symptoms (fever, back pain) for a
  routine bladder infection** — these symptoms indicate the infection
  may have spread and need more urgent attention.

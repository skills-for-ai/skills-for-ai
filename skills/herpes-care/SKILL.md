# Herpes Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Genital herpes is a common sexually transmitted infection caused by
herpes simplex virus (HSV-1 or HSV-2). The virus stays in the body
permanently; outbreaks can recur, though often become less frequent
over time.

## Common symptoms

Painful blisters or sores in the genital area, itching or tingling
before an outbreak, flu-like symptoms during a first outbreak, and pain
with urination. Many people have very mild or no noticeable symptoms.

## General self-care measures

- Take antiviral medication as prescribed, either for active outbreaks
  or as ongoing suppressive therapy if recommended.
- Avoid sexual contact during an active outbreak, when the virus is
  most easily transmitted.
- Use barrier protection (condoms), which reduces but doesn't eliminate
  transmission risk, even without visible symptoms.
- Communicate honestly with sexual partners about your status.

## When to seek medical attention

- A first suspected outbreak, for diagnosis and treatment guidance.
- Frequent or unusually severe outbreaks.
- Herpes during pregnancy — needs specific management to reduce
  transmission risk to the baby.
- Symptoms near the eyes, or in someone with a weakened immune system.

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming no symptoms means no risk of transmission** — the virus
  can shed and transmit even without visible sores (asymptomatic
  shedding).
- **Feeling that a herpes diagnosis is uniquely shameful** — it's a
  very common infection; stigma often causes more distress than the
  physical condition itself, and open communication with a doctor and
  partners is more protective than silence.
- **Stopping suppressive therapy without discussing it** — if prescribed
  for frequent outbreaks, ongoing therapy is often more effective than
  intermittent treatment.

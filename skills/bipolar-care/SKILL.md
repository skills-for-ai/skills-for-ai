# Bipolar Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

Bipolar disorder is a mental health condition characterized by episodes
of depression and episodes of mania or hypomania (elevated mood,
energy, and activity), with periods of relatively stable mood between
episodes for many people.

## Common symptoms

**Depressive episodes:** low mood, loss of interest, fatigue, sleep and
appetite changes (see [[depression-care]]). **Manic/hypomanic episodes:**
elevated or irritable mood, decreased need for sleep, rapid speech,
racing thoughts, increased activity or risk-taking, and impulsivity.

## General self-care measures

- Take mood-stabilizing medication consistently, even when feeling
  well — stopping during a stable period is a common trigger for
  relapse.
- Maintain a regular sleep schedule — sleep disruption is a common
  trigger for mood episodes in both directions.
- Track mood patterns to recognize early warning signs of an episode.
- Build a crisis/relapse plan with your care team in advance, including
  who to contact and what steps to take.

## When to seek medical attention

- Early signs of a manic or depressive episode.
- Significant impulsivity or risk-taking behaviour during a manic
  episode.
- Any thoughts of self-harm or suicide during a depressive episode —
  seek immediate help; see [[urgent-emergency-care]].
- Medication side effects or concerns.

## Common pitfalls

- **Stopping medication once mood stabilizes** — this is one of the
  most common causes of relapse; medication for bipolar disorder is
  typically maintained long-term, not just during episodes.
- **Missing early warning signs of an episode** — mood tracking helps
  catch a developing episode earlier, when intervention is often more
  effective.
- **Treating a manic episode as simply "feeling great"** — mania can
  feel good subjectively while causing real harm (financial,
  relational, occupational); it needs to be recognized and managed, not
  just enjoyed.

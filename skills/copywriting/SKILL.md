# Copywriting

Copywriting is the art and skill of writing persuasive and engaging
content to promote a product, service, or idea — crafting written
material that grabs attention, communicates a message effectively, and
motivates the reader to take a desired action, such as making a
purchase, signing up for a newsletter, or sharing information.

## Key aspects

- **Understand the audience** — know the target audience's needs,
  desires, challenges, and motivations before writing a word.
- **Write compelling hooks** — make the headline and opening sentence
  intriguing and relevant, to earn the reader's attention immediately.
- **Emphasize the Unique Selling Proposition (USP)** — focus on what
  actually differentiates the product, service, or idea from
  alternatives.
- **Persuasive language and tone** — use language and tone that
  resonates with the target audience: evoke emotion, use storytelling,
  incorporate sensory details.
- **Provide a call to action (CTA)** — give a clear, compelling prompt
  for the reader to take a specific next action: sign up, purchase, or
  share.
- **Iterate** — edit, proofread, test, and optimize so the content is
  polished, professional, error-free, readable, credible, and
  effective.

## Copywriting vs. microcopy

Copywriting is typically longer-form and persuasion-focused — ads,
landing pages, marketing emails — aimed at motivating a reader toward a
decision. [[microcopy]] is shorter and function-focused — button labels,
error messages, tooltips — aimed at guiding a user through an
interaction they're already having, rather than persuading them to
start one. Both benefit from understanding the audience and using clear,
purposeful language, but they serve different moments in a user's
journey.

## Common pitfalls

- **Leading with features instead of benefits** — describing what a
  product does rather than what it means for the reader tends to be
  less persuasive; connect features to the outcome the audience actually
  cares about.
- **A weak or missing call to action** — even compelling copy that
  doesn't tell the reader clearly what to do next often fails to convert
  interest into action.
- **Generic language that could apply to any product** — copy that
  doesn't articulate a specific, differentiated USP blends into
  competitors' equally generic messaging.
- **Skipping the iteration step** — publishing a first draft without
  testing or refining it against real audience response leaves easy
  improvements on the table.

# Cold Sore Care

> ⚠️ **Ask your doctor or health care professional. This information is AI generated for education and NOT medical advice.**

A cold sore is a small, painful blister that typically appears on or
around the lips, caused by the herpes simplex virus (HSV-1). The virus
stays in the body permanently and can reactivate periodically.

## Common symptoms

A tingling or burning sensation before a sore appears, followed by
fluid-filled blisters that burst, crust over, and heal over roughly
7–10 days.

## General self-care measures

- Apply antiviral cream early, at the first tingling sensation, if this
  is your typical pattern — earlier treatment tends to be more
  effective.
- Avoid touching the sore and wash hands after any contact to avoid
  spreading the virus.
- Avoid sharing items that touch the mouth (cups, lip balm, cutlery)
  while a sore is present.
- Avoid kissing or oral contact while a sore is active — the virus is
  contagious during outbreaks.
- Identify and manage personal triggers (stress, illness, sun exposure)
  if they seem to bring on outbreaks.

## When to seek medical attention

- Frequent or unusually severe outbreaks.
- A sore that doesn't heal within the usual timeframe or shows signs of
  bacterial infection (increasing redness, pus, spreading warmth).
- Cold sores near the eye — this needs prompt evaluation to protect
  vision.
- A weakened immune system with a cold sore outbreak.

## Common pitfalls

- **Waiting until the blister is fully formed to start treatment** —
  antiviral cream is generally most effective when started at the first
  tingling sensation, before the blister appears.
- **Assuming cold sores are unrelated to genital herpes** — both are
  caused by herpes simplex virus (HSV-1 or HSV-2) and can, in some
  circumstances, spread between sites via contact.
- **Continuing close contact activities while a sore is active** —
  the virus is contagious during an active outbreak, particularly while
  blisters are present.

# Threshold Effects

Threshold effects refer to the point at which a certain condition or
level is reached, causing a significant and often non-linear change or
impact on a business or its operations — a tipping point or critical
threshold that, once crossed, leads to substantial consequences.

## Examples

- **Economies of scale** — expanding production to a certain level can
  cross a threshold where cost per unit starts dropping significantly
  as volume increases (see [[strategic-scale-effects]]).
- **Market penetration** — crossing a critical market share or customer
  base can trigger accelerated growth, increased brand recognition, and
  [[strategic-network-effects]].
- **Technology adoption** — a critical mass of users or stakeholders
  embracing a new technology can trigger widespread adoption,
  interoperability improvements, and increased value for all
  participants.
- **Innovation diffusion** — an innovative product or idea may struggle
  initially, but once it reaches a critical level of acceptance, it can
  spread quickly and disrupt existing norms.
- **Regulatory compliance** — reaching a certain size or revenue
  threshold, or a change in requirements, can trigger new regulatory
  scrutiny, compliance costs, or legal obligations.

## Why this matters strategically

Threshold effects mean that progress toward a goal isn't always
linear — a business can invest steadily with little visible payoff
until a critical threshold is crossed, after which results can change
rapidly. Recognizing this pattern helps avoid abandoning a strategy too
early, right before it would have crossed its threshold, as well as
recognizing when a threshold has genuinely been reached versus assumed.

## Common pitfalls

- **Giving up just before a threshold is reached** — since progress can
  look flat right up until a tipping point, it's easy to mistake
  pre-threshold effort for a failing strategy.
- **Assuming every initiative has a threshold to cross** — not every
  strategy produces a non-linear payoff; some genuinely do scale
  linearly (or not at all), and treating every slow start as
  "pre-threshold" can justify continued investment in something that
  simply isn't working.
- **Missing a regulatory threshold until it's triggered** — growing
  past a size or revenue threshold without proactively tracking
  applicable compliance requirements can create unexpected legal or
  financial exposure.
